# Project Underdog - OHLCV Non-Hostile Transition and Leadership Rotation Candidate Concept Generation v1

## SECTION 1 - Executive Summary

This note generates the first concept inventory for the OHLCV Non-Hostile Transition and Leadership Rotation alpha family. It is concept generation only. No formulas were defined, no code was implemented, no candidate panels were generated, no discovery was executed, no IC was calculated, no redundancy screening was run, no refinement was run, no validation was run, no governance was modified, no thresholds were changed, nothing was registered to production, and no ML was implemented.

Generation objective:

- Create a balanced, economically motivated inventory of exploratory concepts for non-hostile leadership rotation.
- Preserve the distinction from hostile/stress repair, post-drawdown persistence, rank-coherence, ordinary momentum, and PIT/peer-relative research.
- Prepare a concept set that can later be converted into implementation-ready candidate definitions by a separate task.

Number of concepts:

- Initial concepts generated: 10.
- Recommended discovery subset: 9.
- One concept is recommended for merge rather than standalone advancement due overlap with another participation-confirmation concept.

Category balance:

- Orderly leadership emergence: 2 concepts.

- Gradual participation expansion: 2 concepts.
- Rotation acceleration: 1 concept.

- Healthy breadth transitions: 1 concept.

Economic diversity:

The inventory spans leadership emergence, orderly capital migration, trend handoff, participation expansion, rotation speed, leadership confirmation, breadth transition, and post-transition leadership persistence. No single mechanism dominates the initial inventory.

Expected next phase:

The next phase should be a candidate implementation design/specification task that converts the recommended subset into an implementation-ready registry and metadata schema. It should not yet generate panels or run discovery.

## SECTION 7 - Risks

Hidden momentum bias:

- Highest for `nhlr_01`, `nhlr_04`, and `nhlr_07`.
- Mitigation: later implementation must distinguish leadership transition quality from raw recent strength.

Hidden trend-following bias:

- Highest for `nhlr_04` and `nhlr_07`.
- Mitigation: concepts should require transition/handoff interpretation, not simple trend continuation.

Hidden stress-repair bias:

- Highest for `nhlr_05`, `nhlr_09`, and `nhlr_10` because participation and breadth language can drift into repair.
- Mitigation: later implementation must prohibit hostile, panic, weak-breadth repair, and drawdown-recovery activation as primary thesis.

Conceptual redundancy:

- Highest for `nhlr_06`, with overlap against `nhlr_02` and `nhlr_09`.
- Mitigation: merge/remove `nhlr_06`.

Weak economic intuition:

- Highest for `nhlr_08` if framed only as avoiding deceleration.
- Mitigation: define it later as a leadership-quality or rotation-phase control, not a short-reversal concept.

PIT/peer-relative leakage risk:

- Low in this note, because no sector, industry, peer, or PIT metadata is introduced.
- Future implementation must preserve that boundary.
