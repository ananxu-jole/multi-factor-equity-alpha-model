## SECTION 2 - Executive Findings

Strong evidence:

- VoV produced stronger research outcomes than DPath and Event Clustering because it translated more directly from OHLCV observations into measurable states.
- Frozen primary horizons prevented parked modules from being rescued by h1-only, h5-only, or h20-only fragments.
- Negative evidence was preserved effectively in both parked modules.
- Bounded refinement helped VoV only because original module evidence already contained coherent candidate-level support.
- Event Clustering did not support h5/h10 event-topology alpha under the frozen OHLCV-only design.
- DPath did not produce broad, durable h5/h10 evidence under its frozen OHLCV-only design.

Moderate evidence:

- Cleaner mechanism-to-observable mapping appears associated with stronger module outcomes.
- Modules with fewer interpretive layers appear easier to measure and refine.
- Anchor-relative improvement was more useful than raw candidate ranking alone.
- Successful candidates benefited from enough activation density to support review without becoming continuous proxies.
- Parked modules suffered from horizon mismatch and insufficient incremental information beyond contamination references.

Early pattern / hypothesis only:

- Conceptual complexity may be inversely related to empirical success in OHLCV-only research.
- Mechanisms that require latent narrative states, such as market memory or path sequence interpretation, may need external metadata or cleaner anchors to be measurable.
- Future successful modules may need to be closer to direct OHLCV state measurement than to abstract topology.

Unknown:

- Whether DPath or Event Clustering would work with non-OHLCV data.
- Whether peer-relative or economic-context framing would rescue any event or dispersion concepts.
- Whether VoV will remain robust after full contamination-reference review and registration governance.
- Whether current validation-worthy VoV candidates remain independent from volatility compression, hostile/stress repair, rank stability, reversal, or volume-shock references.

## SECTION 4 - What Distinguished VoV

Empirically supported:

- VoV had positive primary-horizon evidence in its validated refined candidates.
- VoV refinement improved branch anchors at the predeclared primary horizons.
- VoV candidates retained positive recent-window and second-half primary-horizon evidence during validation.
- VoV had enough active coverage for research inventory registration review.

Suggestive:

- VoV appears closer to directly measurable OHLCV state behavior than DPath or Event Clustering.
- VoV benefited from a clearer mechanism-to-observable path: volatility instability, chop, and calming are closer to available OHLCV data than latent event memory or multi-step disagreement narratives.
- VoV refinement opportunities were cleaner because they adjusted already-supported branches rather than trying to rescue failed mechanisms.
- VoV's strongest candidates had interpretable anchor-relative improvement, while parked modules mostly had isolated clues or negative evidence.

Unresolved:

- VoV contamination remains a serious open issue. Validation artifacts reported contamination references as placeholder-only.
- VoV registration review is not production deployment.
- VoV may still overlap with volatility compression, hostile/stress repair, persistence/rank stability, plain reversal, volume-shock reversal, or watch-only VoV-like behavior.

Meta-analysis judgment:

- VoV was not simply "successful because volatility matters." It was successful because the module kept a direct measurement target, produced positive primary-horizon evidence, improved anchors through bounded refinement, and retained enough stability and coverage to proceed.

## SECTION 5 - Hypothesis Quality Across Modules

Direct enough to measure cleanly:

- VoV: direct enough. Volatility-instability path behavior is observable in OHLCV data without requiring too many latent intermediate states.
- Participation/stress repair references: already strong as contextual benchmarks because their repair/stabilization behavior has repeated evidence.

Too abstract or too indirect:

- Event Clustering: event topology required event detection, event-type counting, clustering, absorption, deterioration, aging, and memory interpretation. The chain was scientifically coherent but too indirect for positive h5/h10 evidence in this implementation.
- DPath: dispersion sequence was conceptually distinct, but the path narrative was difficult to translate into stable h5/h10 evidence.

Too contaminated:

- Event Clustering had high contamination exposure to volume shock reversal, isolated-event anchors, stress repair, volatility behavior, persistence, non-hostile transition, and static dispersion.
- DPath had contamination risk from static dispersion, low extension, volatility/stress, persistence, and rank coherence.
- VoV still has unresolved contamination risk, but positive primary evidence survived anchor comparisons and validation checks.

Too sparse:

- DPath relapse resilience was relatively sparse and had partial h10 evidence, but positive-rate and durability were insufficient.
- Event Clustering fragmentation had sparse activation and did not produce positive primary evidence.
- VoV strict chop was selective, but not too sparse for validation.

Too horizon-sensitive:

- Event Clustering: h1 was strongest for all candidates, but still negative and not predeclared.
- DPath: one h5 clue failed h10; one h10 clue lacked h20 durability and positive-rate support.
- VoV: h10/h20 alignment was cleaner, with branch-specific primary horizons preserved.

## SECTION 6 - Recurring Contamination Risks

Strong evidence:

- Volatility, stress repair, reversal, persistence/rank stability, and static dispersion recurred as major contamination references.
- Parked modules repeatedly needed to distinguish conceptual novelty from incremental information beyond anchors.
- Anchor-relative interpretation mattered: raw novelty without anchor improvement was not enough.

Moderate evidence:

- Volume shock reversal and isolated-event behavior are especially dangerous for event-like modules.
- Low-extension and stress proxies can masquerade as path or absorption mechanisms.
- Rank coherence and persistence can explain apparent resilience or absorption unless explicitly controlled.

Early pattern / hypothesis only:

- Many OHLCV-only modules may be drawn toward a small set of latent primitives: volatility instability, stress repair, reversal/overextension, rank persistence, liquidity/participation repair, and dispersion level.

Unknown:

- Whether external point-in-time metadata would separate these mechanisms more cleanly.

## SECTION 7 - Horizon Evidence

Supported horizons:

- VoV: h10 and h20 were supported in the validated refined candidates. `vov_03_ref_strict_chop` was h10-led; `vov_01_ref_smoothed_calm` was h20-led with positive h10 support.

Contradicted horizons:

- Event Clustering: h5/h10 primary hypotheses were contradicted. Every candidate was strongest at h1, but h1 remained negative.
- DPath: broad h5/h10 expectation was not robustly supported. `dpath_01` had partial h10 evidence but weak positive-rate and no h20 durability; `dpath_03` had h5-only behavior and failed h10.

Horizon discipline lesson:

- Frozen horizons were essential. Without them, DPath could have been overread through isolated h5/h10 clues and Event Clustering could have been misread through h1-less-negative behavior.

## SECTION 10 - Limits Of OHLCV-Only Research

Strong evidence:

- OHLCV can measure volatility structure, stress/repair behavior, rank persistence, and some stabilization states with enough fidelity for candidate-level research.
- OHLCV-only event topology did not produce positive h5/h10 evidence in the completed Event Clustering module.
- OHLCV-only dispersion path-dependence did not produce robust h5/h10/h20 evidence in its completed first batch.

Moderate evidence:

- OHLCV is strongest when the mechanism is close to directly observable price, range, volatility, liquidity, or rank behavior.
- OHLCV is weaker when the mechanism requires latent intent, event causality, fundamental identity, peer identity, or multi-stage market narratives.
- External metadata may be necessary to distinguish true peer-relative behavior, sector context, structural liquidity, or event meaning.

Unknown:

- Whether carefully designed non-OHLCV data would revive event topology or dispersion path concepts.
- Whether point-in-time peer-relative metadata can produce independent alpha without overfitting.
