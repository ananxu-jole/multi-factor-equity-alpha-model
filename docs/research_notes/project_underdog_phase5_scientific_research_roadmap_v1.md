## Executive Overview

Phase 5 exists because Project Underdog has reached the end of the OHLCV-only era as the primary scientific frontier. Earlier phases proved that disciplined research can find useful evidence in directly measurable state-transition mechanisms, especially hostile/stress repair, participation/breadth repair, persistence, rank structure, volatility compression, and VoV. They also proved that abstract OHLCV-only stories, such as event topology, broad dispersion path narratives, and non-hostile leadership rotation, often fail under frozen horizons and contamination review.

The scientific mission of Phase 5 is to determine whether authoritative point-in-time external information can add independent scientific knowledge beyond own-security OHLCV behavior without weakening the project's evidence discipline.

Expected knowledge gains:

- Whether external information can become a source-authoritative research substrate.
- Whether security identity, ticker lineage, listing status, company-security linkage, economic classification history, size context, peer membership, and event context are scientifically usable.
- Whether peer-relative behavior distinguishes idiosyncratic repair from broad sector, industry, size, or market recovery.
- Whether existing OHLCV successes remain distinct after economic-context controls.
- Which external-information concepts, sources, or peer hypotheses fail and should be preserved as negative evidence.
- What scientific conditions must exist before future ML can be considered.

Relationship to previous phases:

- Phase 5 extends prior work; it does not replace OHLCV evidence, validation methodology, Platform v2, or negative archives.
- Existing OHLCV families become baselines, contamination references, and scientific comparators.
- Previous failures become research constraints that prevent false novelty.

Relationship to future phases:

- Phase 5 should create conditions for a later Phase 6 by maturing external-information knowledge, peer/economic-context hypotheses, contamination understanding, and information-family inventory.
- Phase 5 does not design Phase 6 and does not introduce ML.

## Scientific Mission

The central scientific mission of Phase 5 is:

Determine whether authoritative, point-in-time, reproducible external information adds independent explanatory or predictive structure to Project Underdog beyond validated and archived OHLCV evidence.

Phase 5 should discover knowledge about:

- what makes an external information role scientifically authoritative;
- whether historical identity and lineage can support valid research;
- whether peer-relative and economic-context behavior are independent from broad OHLCV state effects;
- whether external context clarifies prior positive and negative OHLCV results;
- whether future ML could eventually learn independent structure instead of leakage, stress-state concentration, or static-metadata artifacts.

Phase 5 should not seek implementation volume. Its output is scientific understanding, not pipelines.

## Core Research Questions

### Fundamental Questions

- What evidence is required before an external information role can be treated as scientifically authoritative?
- Can point-in-time security identity, ticker lineage, listing status, company-security linkage, and economic classification history be established without look-ahead, survivorship, ticker-reuse, or false-continuity contamination?
- Does peer-relative behavior add independent information beyond own-security OHLCV, market state, stress repair, participation repair, VoV, volatility compression, persistence, and rank-coherence?
- Can economic context separate idiosyncratic repair from broad sector, industry, size, or market recovery?
- Does external information create genuinely new scientific families, or mostly improve contamination control for existing families?
- What evidence must exist before Phase 6 or future ML research becomes scientifically defensible?

### Secondary Questions

- Which external-information roles are mandatory, conditional, diagnostic-only, or optional?
- Are persistence and rank-coherence partly economic-peer effects rather than broad cross-sectional effects?
- Does VoV remain distinct after peer/economic-context and volatility-compression contamination review?
- Can participation/liquidity repair be distinguished from peer-wide liquidity recovery?
- Can dispersion or event concepts become interpretable only after economic or event context exists?
- Which failed OHLCV ideas are true failures, and which are unresolved because OHLCV alone cannot observe the relevant state?

### Exploratory Questions

- Could peer-relative dispersion, event-context interpretation, or economically conditioned leadership become useful future hypotheses?
- Could external information primarily improve falsification rather than produce standalone alpha?
- Which broader future domains, such as macro, options, fixed income, or alternative data, deserve later consideration after equity metadata maturity?
- Could external-information negative evidence become as valuable as positive alpha evidence by closing invalid research paths?

## Scientific Workstreams

The Phase 5 research portfolio should consist of conceptually independent workstreams. They interact through knowledge dependencies, not implementation dependencies.

### WS1 External Information Authority Science

Objective:

Define what makes an external information source or source role scientifically usable.

Scientific motivation:

Repository evidence shows external-information paths remain blocked by license, entitlement, documentation, retention, archive, reproducibility, and date-semantics gaps. No source should become authoritative by convenience.

Expected knowledge contribution:

- Source-role authority criteria.
- Accepted, conditional, rejected, and diagnostic-only source-role logic.
- A fail-closed standard for external information use.

Dependencies:

- Prior source-gate, license, entitlement, PIT, and Platform v2 notes.
- No dependency on any specific external vendor.

Uncertainty addressed:

- Whether any external information can enter the scientific program at all.
- Whether reproducibility is compatible with license and retention constraints.

Expected scientific deliverables:

- Source-authority framework.
- Evidence sufficiency review.
- Negative-source evidence archive.

Success criteria:

- The project can classify information roles as scientifically usable, conditionally usable, diagnostic-only, or rejected based on evidence.

Failure criteria:

- Authority cannot be established, rights are incompatible with reproducible research, or source date semantics remain unknowable.

Interaction with other workstreams:

- Mandatory predecessor for source-dependent conclusions in every later workstream.

### WS2 PIT Identity And Lineage Science

Objective:

Define the scientific requirements for security identity, ticker lineage, listing status, delisting status, share-class treatment, company-security linkage, and corporate continuity.

Scientific motivation:

Peer-relative science fails if ticker-only, current-only, or survivor-biased identity is used. Repository notes repeatedly identify security-master and ticker-lineage gaps as critical blockers.

Expected knowledge contribution:

- Required identity facts for valid historical research.
- Scientific distinction between security identity, issuer identity, ticker label, and economic entity.
- Known failure modes from ticker reuse, false continuity, delisting omission, and current-ticker leakage.

Dependencies:

- WS1 for source authority.
- Existing PIT/source-gate planning notes.

Uncertainty addressed:

- Whether historical observations can be joined to external context without corrupting identity.

Expected scientific deliverables:

- Identity and lineage requirement note.
- False-continuity and survivorship contamination map.
- Mandatory versus optional identity-role taxonomy.

Success criteria:

- The project knows which identity facts are required before peer/economic-context research can be interpreted.

Failure criteria:

- Stable identity, listing history, or known-date lineage cannot be evidenced.

Interaction with other workstreams:

- Enables WS3 economic context validity and WS4 peer-relative hypothesis science.

### WS3 Economic Context Validity Science

Objective:

Determine what makes sector, industry, subindustry, size, market-cap, listing, event, and classification context point-in-time valid.

Scientific motivation:

Current economic metadata is diagnostically useful but static-snapshot only. The readiness audit shows coverage is no longer the primary blocker; historical integrity is.

Expected knowledge contribution:

- Definition of PIT-valid economic context.
- Separation of descriptive diagnostics from validation-quality context.
- Understanding of classification drift, static-label leakage, size leakage, peer-group drift, and fallback false precision.

Dependencies:

- WS1 for source role authority.
- WS2 for historical identity.

Uncertainty addressed:

- Whether economic categories can be historically valid or are contaminated by static/current metadata.

Expected scientific deliverables:

- Economic-context validity framework.
- Context-role maturity taxonomy.
- Static-metadata negative-evidence summary.

Success criteria:

- Economic context roles are classified as mandatory, conditional, diagnostic-only, optional, or blocked.

Failure criteria:

- Classifications are static, backfilled, too sparse, or not reproducible.

Interaction with other workstreams:

- Enables peer-relative hypotheses and contamination controls.

### WS4 Peer-Relative Hypothesis Science

Objective:

Define source-agnostic, falsifiable peer-relative scientific questions without formulas or candidate allocation.

Scientific motivation:

Cross-module meta-analysis selected peer-relative/economic-context readiness as the highest-value next frontier because it addresses the largest missing independent information axis.

Expected knowledge contribution:

- Mechanism-specific peer-relative hypothesis families.
- Scientific ranking of peer-relative post-stress repair, peer rank persistence, industry-relative liquidity stabilization, sector-relative volatility normalization, peer dispersion, and economically conditioned leadership concepts.
- Clear separation between source-independent scientific questions and future source-dependent empirical work.

Dependencies:

- Conceptually depends on WS2 and WS3.
- Empirical future use depends on WS1 authority.

Uncertainty addressed:

- Which peer-relative questions are worth testing once authoritative context exists.

Expected scientific deliverables:

- Peer-relative hypothesis framework.
- Prioritized mechanism queue.
- Falsification expectations and negative learning objectives.

Success criteria:

- Hypotheses are narrow, falsifiable, Platform v2 compatible, and distinct from existing OHLCV families.

Failure criteria:

- Hypotheses collapse into existing repair, rank, persistence, VoV, reversal, or volatility-compression families.

Interaction with other workstreams:

- Consumes WS2/WS3 knowledge; feeds WS5 contamination science and WS7 inventory integration.

### WS5 External-Information Contamination And Orthogonality Science

Objective:

Define how external information could clarify or contaminate existing families.

Scientific motivation:

Prior parked modules show that conceptual novelty is insufficient. Evidence must survive anchor, horizon, contamination, and orthogonality review.

Expected knowledge contribution:

- Contamination map between external context and OHLCV families.
- Scientific distinction between independent information, relabeled state exposure, and improved diagnostics.
- Explicit treatment of negative evidence as a constraint.

Dependencies:

- Existing OHLCV family evidence.
- WS3 and WS4 for context roles and peer hypotheses.

Uncertainty addressed:

- Whether external context adds information or merely describes known stress, volatility, rank, reversal, or repair behavior.

Expected scientific deliverables:

- Cross-family contamination framework.
- Orthogonality review plan at the scientific level.
- Negative-evidence protection language for future modules.

Success criteria:

- Future external-information claims have clear conceptual comparators before empirical work begins.

Failure criteria:

- External features cannot be separated from existing mechanisms, or orthogonality claims depend on post-hoc interpretation.

Interaction with other workstreams:

- Mandatory before any later claim of independent Phase 5 information gain.

### WS6 Negative Evidence And Falsification Science

Objective:

Preserve rejected sources, unusable metadata roles, failed hypotheses, blocked semantics, and non-independent context effects as scientific knowledge.

Scientific motivation:

Platform v2 improved the project by preserving negative evidence from DPath, Event Clustering, and leadership rotation instead of rescuing weak modules.

Expected knowledge contribution:

- Prevents repetition of invalid or already-failed concepts.
- Converts source blockers and failed hypotheses into durable constraints.
- Keeps Phase 5 from becoming broad external-feature search.

Dependencies:

- Runs continuously across all workstreams.

Uncertainty addressed:

- Which ideas, sources, and metadata roles should not be recycled as novelty.

Expected scientific deliverables:

- Negative-evidence review notes.
- Blocked-source and blocked-role records.
- Parked-hypothesis interpretation summaries.

Success criteria:

- Failed work remains interpretable and reusable as boundary knowledge.

Failure criteria:

- Failed or blocked ideas are left ambiguous and later reintroduced without a new hypothesis.

Interaction with other workstreams:

- Protects all workstreams from false rescue and scope creep.

### WS7 Existing-Family Reinterpretation Science

Objective:

Use Phase 5 concepts to reinterpret, not retest, prior OHLCV evidence.

Scientific motivation:

Existing families are the baseline evidence system. Peer/economic context may explain whether repair, persistence, rank, VoV, dispersion, event, and transition findings were idiosyncratic, broad-market, peer-wide, or contaminated.

Expected knowledge contribution:

- Scientific map of which prior results external information may clarify.
- Identification of existing families that are likely independent versus likely context-dependent.
- Better long-term portfolio knowledge without changing prior governance outcomes.

Dependencies:

- Existing family archives.
- WS4 and WS5 for peer and contamination framing.

Uncertainty addressed:

- Whether previous evidence represents true independent behavior or broad economic-state exposure.

Expected scientific deliverables:

- Cross-family reinterpretation framework.
- Existing-family context-dependency map.
- Archive-preserving synthesis note.

Success criteria:

- Prior evidence is extended without being rewritten, rescued, or governance-mutated.

Failure criteria:

- The work attempts to reopen parked modules or change previous outcomes without a new Platform v2 hypothesis.

Interaction with other workstreams:

- Supports WS7 inventory integration and Phase 6 readiness.

### WS8 Integrated Scientific Information Inventory

Objective:

Create a scientific inventory of accepted, conditional, diagnostic, rejected, and unknown information families.

Scientific motivation:

Future ML or portfolio research requires information-family lineage, not just candidate lists. The repository shows family concentration remains a central limitation.

Expected knowledge contribution:

- Information-family maturity map.
- Separation of OHLCV, external-information, diagnostic, and negative-evidence roles.
- A portfolio-level view of scientific independence and concentration.

Dependencies:

- WS1 through WS7 evidence.

Uncertainty addressed:

- Whether Phase 5 has diversified the knowledge base beyond the stress-repair umbrella and conditional OHLCV satellites.

Expected scientific deliverables:

- Integrated information inventory.
- Family-independence and concentration assessment.
- Phase 6 readiness input.

Success criteria:

- Information families have clear maturity labels and evidence lineage.

Failure criteria:

- The inventory remains dominated by one umbrella or undocumented external roles.

Interaction with other workstreams:

- Precondition for WS9 and future Phase 6 readiness assessment.

### WS9 ML Readiness Science

Objective:

Define the scientific conditions under which future ML could become appropriate, without introducing ML.

Scientific motivation:

Repository audits consistently classify ML as premature because feature diversity, PIT metadata, family independence, and model-governance maturity are insufficient.

Expected knowledge contribution:

- Conceptual preconditions for future model research.
- Leakage and state-concentration warnings.
- Definition of what ML would need to learn independent structure rather than artifacts.

Dependencies:

- WS8 integrated inventory.
- Sufficient maturity in validation and information diversity.

Uncertainty addressed:

- Whether ML should ever follow Phase 5, and what must be true first.

Expected scientific deliverables:

- ML readiness science note.
- Future model-governance research questions.
- Deferred-risk register.

Success criteria:

- ML preconditions are explicit, evidence-based, and not confused with implementation plans.

Failure criteria:

- ML remains unsupported because metadata, validation, diversity, or leakage controls are inadequate.

Interaction with other workstreams:

- Future-facing only; no ML is introduced in Phase 5.

## Dependency Graph

Mandatory predecessors:

- WS1 precedes all source-dependent scientific conclusions.
- WS2 precedes all historical peer, listing, issuer, and security-context interpretation.
- WS3 precedes all economic-context and peer-membership claims.
- WS5 precedes any claim of independent external-information value.
- WS8 precedes WS9 and any Phase 6 readiness assessment.

Parallel work:

- WS1 can proceed with license, entitlement, documentation, retention, and authority evidence.
- WS2 can proceed as source-independent identity requirement science.
- WS3 can proceed as validity criteria and static-metadata limitation science.
- WS5 can begin from existing OHLCV archives before real external evidence exists.
- WS6 runs continuously.
- WS7 can begin as reinterpretation framing without empirical retesting.

Optional work:

- Leadership inversion diagnostics, event-context reframing, dispersion reframing, and narrow direct-state OHLCV follow-through are optional.
- These should not preempt the core Phase 5 external-information program.

Future work:

- Empirical peer-relative modules, external-information validation, broader data-domain expansion, ML readiness design, and portfolio research belong after the required scientific understanding exists.

Scientific bottlenecks:

- Source authority.
- License, entitlement, retention, and reproducibility rights.
- Known-date and effective-date semantics.
- Security identity and ticker lineage.
- Historical economic classification and size context.
- Peer-group validity and historical coverage.

Knowledge bottlenecks:

- Whether peer-relative information is independent.
- Whether economic context clarifies or merely relabels existing OHLCV findings.
- Whether external information can be reproduced and audited under source constraints.
- Whether Phase 5 produces enough information diversity to justify later phases.

## Knowledge-Generation Sequence

Phase 5 should generate knowledge in this order.

1. Define external-information authority.
   - Why it comes first: without authority, downstream scientific claims are assumptions.
   - Uncertainty reduction: source usability, rights, reproducibility, and date semantics.
   - Information gain: high because even rejection clarifies the frontier.
   - Risk reduction: prevents false source authority.

2. Define PIT identity and lineage requirements.
   - Why it follows: peer and economic context need stable historical identity.
   - Uncertainty reduction: ticker reuse, issuer/security ambiguity, delisting and survivorship risk.
   - Information gain: high because identity failure invalidates downstream science.
   - Risk reduction: prevents false joins and current-label contamination.

3. Define economic-context validity.
   - Why it follows: classifications and size context need date-safe interpretation.
   - Uncertainty reduction: static-label leakage, classification drift, peer drift, size leakage.
   - Information gain: high because it determines whether peer research is meaningful.
   - Risk reduction: prevents descriptive metadata from becoming validation evidence.

4. Define peer-relative hypotheses.
   - Why it follows: hypotheses should be metadata-aware but source-agnostic.
   - Uncertainty reduction: which scientific questions are testable and worth preserving.
   - Information gain: high because it prioritizes future modules before formulas exist.
   - Risk reduction: prevents broad search.

5. Define contamination and orthogonality expectations.
   - Why it follows: peer context must be interpreted against existing family evidence.
   - Uncertainty reduction: independent information versus relabeled OHLCV behavior.
   - Information gain: high because it protects future evidence interpretation.
   - Risk reduction: prevents false novelty.

6. Preserve negative evidence continuously.
   - Why it follows and runs throughout: Phase 5 should learn from blockers and failures.
   - Uncertainty reduction: which paths are invalid, blocked, or merely diagnostic.
   - Information gain: medium to high because negative evidence narrows the frontier.
   - Risk reduction: prevents repeated dead ends.

7. Reinterpret existing families scientifically.
   - Why it follows: context and contamination frameworks are needed before reinterpretation.
   - Uncertainty reduction: whether prior OHLCV families are idiosyncratic, broad, peer-wide, or contaminated.
   - Information gain: medium to high because it improves the whole research record.
   - Risk reduction: avoids unearned continuation of parked modules.

8. Build the integrated scientific information inventory.
   - Why it follows: inventory requires accepted, conditional, diagnostic, and rejected evidence.
   - Uncertainty reduction: family diversity and independence.
   - Information gain: high for Phase 6 readiness.
   - Risk reduction: prevents premature ML or portfolio claims.

9. Reassess ML readiness scientifically.
   - Why it comes last: ML should follow information diversity, not substitute for it.
   - Uncertainty reduction: whether model research would learn structure or leakage.
   - Information gain: future-facing.
   - Risk reduction: blocks premature ML.

## Scientific Maturity Model

Phase 5 uses the following maturity vocabulary while retaining compatibility with Platform v2. It does not redefine governance.

| Stage | Meaning | Evidence expected |
|---|---|---|
| Concept | A scientific question or information role is identified. | Motivation and relationship to existing evidence. |
| Hypothesis | A falsifiable knowledge claim is articulated. | Expected contribution, failure mode, and learning objective. |
| Framework | Dependencies, contamination risks, and source-role requirements are defined. | Authority, PIT, identity, context, and orthogonality criteria. |
| Evidence | Documentary, conceptual, or later empirical evidence is collected. | Accepted, conditional, rejected, blocked, or diagnostic status. |
| Validation | Future empirical claims are tested under approved lifecycle standards. | Separate validation evidence, not source acceptance alone. |
| Accepted Knowledge | The project records what is known, unknown, rejected, or reusable. | Research review, inventory update, negative-evidence preservation. |

Phase 5 roadmap maturity target:

- WS1 through WS5 should reach at least Framework maturity.
- WS6 should operate continuously at Evidence maturity for failures and blockers.
- WS7 and WS8 should mature only after enough upstream evidence exists.
- WS9 should remain conceptual until Phase 5 information diversity improves.

## Expected Discoveries

### Likely Discoveries

- Some external information roles will remain blocked by rights, entitlement, documentation, retention, reproducibility, or date semantics.
- Static/current metadata will remain diagnostic-only.
- Identity and lineage will be mandatory foundations.
- Peer-relative hypotheses will need narrower scope than broad prior designs.
- Existing OHLCV families will remain mandatory contamination references.

Scientific value:

These discoveries prevent invalid research before it starts.

### Possible Discoveries

- Peer-relative repair may distinguish idiosyncratic recovery from peer-wide recovery.
- Industry-relative liquidity stabilization may add context beyond participation repair.
- Peer-group rank persistence may explain part of rank-coherence or persistence evidence.
- Sector-relative volatility normalization may clarify VoV and volatility-compression boundaries.
- Economic context may explain why dispersion, event topology, or leadership rotation failed under OHLCV-only designs.

Scientific value:

These discoveries could create new independent information families or strengthen interpretation of existing families.

### Negative Discoveries

- No source path may satisfy authority, rights, reproducibility, or PIT semantics.
- Peer groups may be too sparse, unstable, backfilled, or taxonomy-dependent.
- Economic context may add little independent information beyond existing OHLCV families.
- External information may improve diagnostics but not alpha science.

Scientific value:

Negative evidence protects the research record and prevents sunk-cost continuation.

### Unexpected Discoveries

- External information may mainly function as contamination control rather than standalone feature source.
- Failed OHLCV modules may become more valuable as boundary maps than as future hypotheses.
- Some source families may be useful only for auditability, not for discovery.
- Future ML may remain blocked longer than expected because information diversity is still insufficient.

Scientific value:

Unexpected discoveries reshape long-term priorities without requiring post-hoc rescue.

## Evidence Hierarchy

Phase 5 conclusions should be supported by this evidence hierarchy:

1. Conceptual reasoning: why the question matters.
2. Architectural reasoning: whether the question can be represented without leakage, false identity, or false peer membership.
3. Documentary evidence: source rights, scope, definitions, lineage, date semantics, retention, reproducibility, and publication constraints.
4. Empirical evidence: future approved tests after source and PIT prerequisites are met.
5. Validation evidence: separated future validation under existing lifecycle standards.
6. Negative evidence: rejected sources, failed hypotheses, blocked semantics, and archived parked-module lessons.
7. Cross-workstream evidence: convergence across authority, identity, context, peer hypotheses, contamination, and family reinterpretation.
8. Reproducibility evidence: manifests, lineage records, checksums or controlled references, row counts, and auditability where permitted.

No numerical thresholds are assigned by this roadmap.

## Interaction With Previous Phases

Phase 5 extends previous phases rather than replacing them.

OHLCV families:

- Hostile/stress repair remains the established core umbrella and major contamination benchmark.
- Participation and breadth repair remain strong but concentrated.
- Volatility compression remains useful but repair-adjacent.
- Persistence and rank-coherence remain conditional candidate-lineages, not broad family proof.
- VoV remains the strongest recent positive module, with contamination review still important.

VoV:

- VoV shows that directly measurable structure can survive bounded refinement and validation.
- Phase 5 uses VoV as a model of evidence discipline and as a contamination reference, not as a reason to expand volatility formulas.

Validation:

- Phase 5 preserves separation between discovery, validation, governance, and production.
- Source acceptance is not validation.
- Peer-relative hypotheses are not validation-ready until PIT evidence exists and future lifecycle stages are separately approved.

Platform v2:

- Phase 5 keeps hypothesis-first discipline, orthogonality, falsifiability, learning objective, candidate discipline, and negative-evidence preservation.
- It adds no new governance standard.

Negative evidence:

- DPath, Event Clustering, and leadership rotation remain archived negative evidence.
- Their failure modes should inform Phase 5 rather than be reopened.

Cross-module synthesis:

- The reason Phase 5 exists is the meta-analysis conclusion that the largest next independent axis is peer-relative/economic context, not another broad OHLCV transformation.

## Relationship To Phase 6

Phase 6 should not begin until Phase 5 creates sufficient scientific conditions.

Required scientific conditions:

- Knowledge maturity: source authority, PIT semantics, identity, lineage, context validity, and peer hypotheses are accepted, rejected, or explicitly blocked.
- Feature maturity: at least one external-information role has a scientifically valid role, or the project has clearly documented why none can.
- Validation maturity: future external-information claims have a path to validation separate from source acceptance.
- Information diversity: the research portfolio has improved beyond one dominant repair umbrella plus conditional OHLCV satellites, or has clearly documented why it cannot.
- Contamination maturity: external information has been compared conceptually against stress repair, participation repair, VoV, volatility compression, persistence, rank, reversal, dispersion, event, and transition archives.
- Negative-evidence maturity: blocked sources, failed metadata roles, and invalid hypotheses are preserved.
- ML readiness conceptually: model preconditions are explicit, but ML remains deferred unless evidence supports it.

Remaining uncertainty before Phase 6 may still be acceptable if it is named, bounded, and not central to the next phase. Unnamed source, leakage, or peer-validity uncertainty should block Phase 6.

## Opportunity-Cost Assessment

| Effort area | Scientific opportunity cost | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Broad OHLCV family discovery | Delays the highest-value independent axis and risks rebranding stress, reversal, volatility, rank, or repair behavior. | Low priority except for narrow direct-state questions. |
| Implementation-first work | Converts unresolved authority and PIT assumptions into hidden contamination. | Avoid. |
| Governance expansion | Can clarify source authority, but process expansion without evidence decisions slows learning. | Useful only when tied to real evidence bottlenecks. |
| Infrastructure expansion | Valuable after authority decisions, but premature before source roles are accepted. | Defer broad infrastructure. |
| External-information research | Slower near-term alpha output but highest expected uncertainty reduction. | Primary Phase 5 focus. |
| Premature ML | Risks learning static metadata leakage, stress-state concentration, family redundancy, or source artifacts. | Defer. |

Scientific conclusion:

The opportunity cost of not doing Phase 5 is high because the project would remain trapped in an increasingly saturated OHLCV-only search space. The opportunity cost of doing Phase 5 poorly is also high because invalid metadata could corrupt the research record.

## Research Portfolio Balance

The recommended workstreams form a balanced scientific portfolio.

Novelty:

- External information and peer/economic context are the largest underexplored axes in the repository evidence.

Scientific independence:

- Identity, context, peer-relative behavior, contamination science, negative evidence, and ML readiness answer different questions.

Risk diversification:

- The roadmap includes both positive-discovery paths and failure-preserving paths.

Expected information gain:

- Highest in source authority, identity, and economic-context validity because these determine whether the whole frontier is possible.
- High in peer-relative hypothesis science because it defines future modules before implementation pressure exists.
- High in contamination science because it protects against false novelty.

Expected long-term contribution:

- Phase 5 can create the first valid bridge from OHLCV-only evidence to richer information-family science.

Balance caveat:

- The portfolio is intentionally not balanced across every possible external data domain. It is balanced around the current bottleneck: authoritative PIT equity metadata and peer/economic context.

## Optional Programs

The following programs are optional and should not block the core Phase 5 sequence:

- Leadership inversion diagnostic framing, only as a new hypothesis and not as continuation of the rejected leadership family.
- Event-context reframing, only after external event authority is understood.
- Peer-dispersion reframing, only after valid peer groups exist.
- Narrow OHLCV direct-state follow-through, only if it does not delay source-authority and PIT-context science.
- Future broader external-domain scans, only after equity metadata maturity improves.

Optional programs should be classified as diagnostic or future-facing unless they satisfy the same scientific dependency sequence.

## Open Questions

- Whether PIT date semantics are sufficient.
- Whether peer-relative information is independent.

### Long-Term Questions

- Whether future ML becomes scientifically justified.
- Whether portfolio-level research becomes appropriate after integrated information maturity.
