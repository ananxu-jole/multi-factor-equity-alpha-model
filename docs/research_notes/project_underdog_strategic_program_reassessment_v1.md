## Executive Assessment

Project Underdog has reached a natural scientific transition point.

The project is no longer an early alpha-search sandbox. It now has a mature research lifecycle, reproducible artifacts, frozen-horizon discipline, bounded refinement rules, validation separation, negative-evidence preservation, and Platform v2 scientific entry standards. It has also accumulated enough positive and negative evidence to see the shape of the opportunity set.

The strongest scientific achievement is not any single candidate. It is the project's conversion from broad OHLCV exploration into a disciplined evidence machine: direct, measurable state-transition mechanisms can survive; abstract OHLCV-only narratives usually do not; negative results are now interpretable rather than wasted; and source-gate governance prevents unsupported metadata shortcuts.

The highest-value next frontier is peer-relative / economic-context research. However, the project is not ready for peer-relative implementation. The next major phase should be the PIT economic-context evidence and source-gate transition phase: resolving license, entitlement, table/documentation, date-semantics, retention, and source-governance blockers so that peer-relative research can later begin without contaminating the scientific record.

This is a phase transition from OHLCV-only discovery as the main frontier to authoritative metadata readiness as the bottleneck frontier.

## Scientific Knowledge Inventory

### Established

- Hostile/stress repair and participation/breadth/liquidity repair are the strongest recurring scientific umbrella.
- Frozen primary horizons prevent false rescue through post-hoc h1/h5/h20 reinterpretation.
- Bounded refinement is useful only when original evidence is already coherent.
- Static metadata is diagnostic-only and cannot support validation-quality peer-relative research.
- License, entitlement, retention, and source-lineage evidence are prerequisites for PIT metadata use.

### Strong Evidence

- VoV contains candidate-level medium-horizon information after bounded refinement: `vov_03_ref_strict_chop` and `vov_01_ref_smoothed_calm` passed validation and were recommended for registration review, with contamination review pending.
- Event Clustering failed as an OHLCV-only h5/h10 event-topology module.
- Dispersion Path-Dependence was scientifically coherent but empirically weak and horizon-inconsistent.
- Non-hostile leadership rotation failed broadly at h10/h20 as implemented.
- Platform v2 improved the quality of negative evidence and stopped weak modules cleanly.

### Moderate Evidence

- Persistence and rank-coherence are meaningful conditional candidate-lineages, not broad established families.
- OHLCV-only alpha appears strongest when mechanisms are close to measurable volatility, range, liquidity, participation, rank, or repair states.
- Many attempted new OHLCV families collapse back into stress repair, reversal, volatility compression, rank persistence, or event noise.
- Peer-relative context is likely to add the largest independent information axis if PIT metadata can be made authoritative.

### Weak Evidence

- Dispersion re-entry or stabilization may contain isolated clues, but existing evidence does not justify continuation of the parked module.
- Non-hostile leadership inversion may be diagnostically interesting, but not as refinement of the rejected family.
- Participation/liquidity outside repair states remains possible but not yet strong enough as a major frontier.

### Negative Evidence

- Broad transition/leadership rotation under the implemented OHLCV design.
- Event memory, clustering, aging, concentration, and fragmentation as implemented in the Event Clustering module.
- Dispersion path divergence and consensus-without-crowding as implemented in DPath.
- Broad v3/v4 naive continuation and many rank-quality or breakout variants that collapsed into reversal/overextension behavior.

### Unknown

- Whether peer-relative PIT metadata produces independent alpha.
- Whether historical sector/industry/size context separates idiosyncratic repair from market-wide recovery.
- Whether VoV remains independent after full contamination-reference review.
- Whether ML can add value once the family inventory and metadata substrate mature.
- Whether externally licensed sources permit reproducible PIT construction under retention and publication constraints.

## Alpha-Family Assessment

The portfolio is scientifically credible but still concentrated.

The core family remains hostile/stress repair, including participation, breadth, liquidity, and stabilization under adverse states. This is the most mature and repeatedly useful scientific region. It is also the main concentration risk.

Volatility-of-volatility is now the strongest incremental positive family after the core repair umbrella. Its validated refined candidates add a volatility-structure axis, but their contamination review remains incomplete, so the family should be treated as registration-review worthy rather than fully independent.

Persistence and rank-coherence improve diversity. They show that Project Underdog can find conditional candidate-lineage evidence outside direct participation repair. Still, they are not broad established families. Rank-coherence in particular carries stress-repair similarity and sibling redundancy risk.

Dispersion is conceptually independent but empirically weak. Event clustering and leadership rotation are now more valuable as negative-evidence assets than as active families. Economic context and peer-relative behavior remain the largest unexplored structural gap, but they are blocked by PIT data readiness.

Portfolio conclusion: the project has enough family evidence to justify a new major research phase, but not enough independent validated families to justify ML as the next phase.

## Negative-Evidence Assessment

The following should likely remain retired unless restarted by a genuinely new Platform v2 hypothesis:

- Event Clustering as an OHLCV-only event-topology family. It had uniformly negative expected-primary evidence, h1-only less-negative behavior, and high contamination risk.
- Dispersion Path-Dependence as implemented. It produced partial clues but failed family-level h5/h10/h20 support and should not be rescued through a second refinement cycle.
- OHLCV Non-Hostile Transition and Leadership Rotation as implemented. The medium-horizon thesis was broadly negative, with possible inversion only as a separate diagnostic.
- Naive continuation, leadership, broad rank-quality, and overextension-adjacent variants that repeatedly failed or collapsed into reversal/late-entry decay.
- Structural interaction variants that mainly recombine existing repair ingredients without independent evidence.

Theoretically attractive but empirically weak ideas include event memory, consensus formation without crowding, abstract market-disagreement path narratives, and clean non-hostile leadership continuation from OHLCV alone.

These findings should remain in the archive because they prevent false novelty. They also explain why another broad OHLCV concept sweep has lower expected marginal return now than it did earlier.

## Remaining OHLCV Opportunity

Arguments for continued OHLCV exploration:

- OHLCV has produced real evidence in hostile/stress repair, persistence/rank-adjacent behavior, volatility compression, and VoV.
- Directly measurable mechanisms still have room, especially non-hostile volatility adaptation, participation/liquidity outside repair, and narrowly scoped continuation/inversion diagnostics.
- The infrastructure for OHLCV modules is mature, so marginal implementation cost is lower than a new data regime.

Arguments for diminishing returns:

- The strongest recent positives cluster around a small set of primitives: repair, stabilization, volatility structure, rank persistence, and liquidity/participation.
- Major attempted expansions into leadership rotation, dispersion path-dependence, and event topology produced negative or parked results.
- New OHLCV ideas increasingly need elaborate semantic distinctions to avoid rediscovering stress repair, reversal, compression, or rank stability.
- Broad discovery would compete directly with the higher-value metadata frontier.

Expected marginal benefit: moderate for narrow, directly measurable modules; low for broad abstract OHLCV families.

Expected opportunity cost: high if OHLCV discovery delays PIT/economic-context readiness, because the largest missing information axis would remain untouched.

Conclusion: OHLCV-only discovery is not exhausted, but it is near diminishing returns as the primary strategic frontier.

## Peer-Relative Assessment

Scientific importance: very high. Peer-relative context directly addresses the project's largest interpretive limitation: distinguishing idiosyncratic repair from broad sector, industry, size, or market recovery.

Dependency burden: very high. The frontier requires authoritative PIT security identity, ticker lineage, listing/delisting history, sector/industry history, corporate-action lineage, historical size or market-cap support if used, known-date semantics, retention rights, and source-gate governance.

Expected information gain: highest among reviewed frontiers. It could reframe existing successful mechanisms by asking whether repair, rank persistence, volatility stabilization, and participation behavior are unique relative to contemporaneous economic peers.

Expected implementation cost: high. The repository has source-gate and evidence packages, but no source is approved, no entitlement is confirmed, no table inventory exists, and no PIT construction is authorized.

Timing: now for evidence and source-gate transition; later for implementation and alpha discovery after external evidence is accepted.

Conclusion: peer-relative/economic context is the highest-value frontier, but the immediate phase is metadata evidence readiness rather than peer alpha implementation.

## Machine Learning Readiness

ML is not scientifically justified as the next major phase.

Feature maturity: moderate for OHLCV-derived candidate features, weak for PIT/economic metadata, absent for source-approved peer features.

Label maturity: acceptable for research diagnostics and validation artifacts, but not yet broad enough for model training across diverse independent families.

Governance maturity: strong for research modules, validation, and artifact integrity; not yet specialized for ML experiment tracking, model leakage control, feature-store lineage, or model governance.

Validation maturity: strong for candidate-level signal validation; not yet an ML validation framework.

Data maturity: adequate for OHLCV-only research; inadequate for PIT peer/economic features.

Family diversity: insufficient. One established umbrella plus conditional satellites and VoV candidates do not yet provide broad, independent training structure.

Remaining manual-discovery value: still meaningful, especially in source readiness, peer-relative design, and contamination review.

Risk of ML too early: the model would likely learn stress-state exposure, repair/stabilization regimes, turnover artifacts, or data leakage through static metadata.

Risk of ML too late: eventually the project could underuse accumulated candidate panels and cross-family interactions if it waits after several independent families mature.

Possible advantages: nonlinear interaction discovery, dynamic family weighting, contamination diagnostics, and state-conditional blending after metadata and family diversity mature.

Possible disadvantages: reduced interpretability, leakage risk, false confidence, data-snooping pressure, and governance complexity.

Conclusion: ML should remain deferred until the project has broader validated family diversity, accepted PIT metadata if peer features are used, and an explicit ML governance layer.

## Infrastructure Assessment

Infrastructure is no longer the main limiter for OHLCV-only research. Candidate generation, panel generation, IC discovery, validation, reproducibility, manifests, checksum discipline, lifecycle governance, and negative-evidence preservation are mature enough to continue bounded modules.

Infrastructure is the main limiter for the next frontier only through source management and PIT readiness:

- Source-gate scaffolds exist but no real source has passed.
- License and entitlement evidence are absent.
- Retention, archive, checksum, and controlled-reference policies are unresolved for licensed data.
- PIT security master and ticker lineage are architected but not constructed.
- Economic-context metadata is diagnostic/static only.
- Peer groups cannot be built for alpha use until authoritative PIT metadata exists.

Conclusion: the infrastructure gap is not generic engineering. It is external-source governance, entitlement, and PIT metadata readiness.

## Program Risks

- Premature ML: models could amplify concentrated repair/stabilization exposure and static-metadata leakage.
- Late ML: after more families mature, delaying ML too long could miss useful nonlinear interaction diagnostics.
- Over-engineering: source-gate and PIT work could become process-heavy without producing evidence decisions.
- Insufficient diversity: the active inventory remains too concentrated in stress repair and related stabilization.
- Confirmation bias: repeated success in repair states could cause new ideas to be judged by repair-like behavior.
- Governance complexity: the lifecycle is strong, but it can slow learning if every small diagnostic becomes a major phase.
- Dependency bottlenecks: CRSP/WRDS/Compustat/license evidence may block the highest-value frontier.
- Research fatigue: repeated negative OHLCV modules may make the team overcorrect away from useful narrow direct-state research.
- False novelty: new OHLCV names may hide old reversal, rank, volatility, or repair exposure.
- Scientific stagnation: continuing broad OHLCV discovery without new information axes could yield lower marginal learning.

## Recommended Next Phase

Recommend exactly one next major phase:

`PIT Economic-Context Evidence And Source-Gate Transition Phase`

This phase should focus on resolving the institutional, licensing, entitlement, documentation, retention, date-semantics, and source-gate prerequisites for authoritative PIT metadata.

Rationale from repository evidence:

- Cross-module meta-analysis selected peer-relative/economic-context as the best next frontier.
- Alpha-family inventory identifies peer-relative/economic context as the largest independent gap.
- Platform v2 evidence shows abstract OHLCV-only expansion has declining marginal return.
- License and entitlement notes show the project has enough internal clarity to begin bounded external evidence collection, but no source is approved and no entitlement is confirmed.
- PIT readiness notes show implementation must remain blocked until external evidence is accepted.

This recommendation does not authorize source access, PIT construction, peer construction, formulas, candidates, panels, IC, validation, production, thresholds, or ML.

## Long-Term Roadmap

If this recommendation is adopted, the likely one-to-two-year roadmap is:

1. Complete user-led institutional access and entitlement inquiry.
2. Collect approved license, entitlement, documentation, retention, and publication-rights evidence.
3. Review source-family evidence and decide whether any source can enter source-gate design.
4. Perform field-level documentation review only after entitlement and rights are established.
5. Build or update source-gate manifests using evidence, not assumptions.
6. If approved, design PIT security identity and ticker-lineage construction under controlled governance.
7. Only after accepted identity lineage, review sector/industry/company/security linkage and historical size evidence.
8. Only after accepted PIT economic metadata, design peer-group construction and fail-closed eligibility rules.
9. Only after reproducible PIT peer groups exist, activate the first peer-relative module design.
10. Reassess ML readiness after at least one independent peer-relative family or additional validated non-repair family exists.

This roadmap is conceptual. It does not design future implementations.

## Final Classification

Final classification: `PROJECT_READY_FOR_NEXT_MAJOR_PHASE`

Interpretation:

- Project Underdog is mature enough to leave broad OHLCV-only discovery as the primary frontier.
- The project is not blocked from all progress, because it can now proceed into evidence and source-gate transition work.
- The highest-value scientific frontier remains blocked from implementation until external evidence is accepted.
- ML is not yet scientifically justified as the next major phase.
