## 2. Primary Scientific Objective

Determine whether a security's repair, stabilization, persistence, or deterioration relative to an authoritative point-in-time economic peer group contains forward information beyond its own OHLCV history, broad market and sector state, validated volatility-of-volatility structure, and existing participation, stress-repair, rank, and persistence families.

This objective is scientifically attractive because the completed OHLCV-only module cycle showed that mechanisms tied directly to measurable state transitions can produce useful evidence, while abstract topology, broad path dependence, and unanchored event clustering tend to collapse into contamination, horizon mismatch, or weak anchor-relative differentiation.

The frontier therefore should not ask whether peer labels are useful in general. It should ask whether valid historical peer context separates idiosyncratic repair from broad recovery, durable stabilization from market-wide compression, and peer-relative persistence from ordinary rank or self-history.

## 3. Why This Is The Highest-Value Next Frontier

The cross-module meta-analysis selected peer-relative / economic-context design as the next frontier because it addresses a recurring limitation of pure OHLCV research: own-security price, range, and volume can describe what happened, but often cannot identify whether the behavior was idiosyncratic, sector-wide, industry-wide, size-driven, or part of a broad stress regime.

Evidence from completed modules supports this direction:

- Validated volatility-of-volatility research showed that tightly anchored, directly measurable OHLCV mechanisms can work when frozen horizons and contamination controls are respected.
- Dispersion path-dependence produced coherent but weak and horizon-inconsistent evidence, suggesting that relative or cross-sectional structure needs stronger economic anchoring before it can be interpreted.
- Event Clustering produced a trustworthy negative result, showing that abstract event topology alone did not add durable information under OHLCV-only data.
- Across modules, weak anchor improvement, horizon mismatch, and contamination by broad stress or reversal effects were recurring failure modes.

Inference from that evidence: point-in-time peer and economic context is the most plausible next dimension because it can test whether successful OHLCV state-transition ideas remain useful after separating issuer-specific behavior from valid contemporaneous peer behavior. That inference remains conditional on licensed, reproducible PIT metadata.

## 4. OHLCV-Only Limitations This Frontier May Address

Evidence established by completed research:

- Some OHLCV-only mechanisms were too abstract to survive empirical testing.
- Several families showed horizon mismatch, especially when apparent short-horizon behavior did not support the original primary horizon.
- Broad stress, volatility compression, reversal, rank coherence, and persistence frequently contaminated candidate interpretation.
- Event topology did not reliably distinguish durable clustering from rapidly absorbed market information.
- Static dispersion and anchor-relative comparisons were often insufficient to prove economic distinctiveness.

Scientific inference for the frontier:

- PIT peer context may separate idiosyncratic repair from broad sector or industry recovery.
- Historical industry and subindustry groupings may clarify whether liquidity stabilization is security-specific or merely a contemporaneous peer effect.
- Valid sector-relative volatility normalization may distinguish useful normalization from generic VoV or volatility compression.
- Peer-rank behavior after drawdown may test whether rank persistence has economic structure beyond existing rank coherence and persistence families.
- Cross-peer dispersion behavior may be interpretable only when peer groups are historically valid and not static snapshots.
- Economic context may reduce false positives caused by static-dispersion ambiguity, event-topology contamination, and weak anchor-relative differentiation.

These are hypotheses to frame, not conclusions to act on. They require authoritative PIT metadata before measurable candidate work can begin.

## 5. Status Of Existing Peer-Relative Design

The existing Peer-Relative Repair/Stabilization/Asymmetry design remains scientifically current as a source document, but it is too broad to activate as a single Platform v2 module.

Preserve:

- The emphasis on repair, stabilization, asymmetry, persistence, and peer-relative conditioning.
- The requirement that static metadata is diagnostic only and cannot support alpha discovery.
- The dependency on PIT security identity, ticker lineage, sector and industry history, listing status, corporate-action lineage, and historical size context.
- The fail-closed posture for missing or ambiguous economic metadata.

Revise before activation:

- Split the broad frontier into smaller mechanism-specific modules.
- Treat prior concept groupings as scientific themes, not candidate allocations.
- Remove any future candidate-like labeling until the source gate clears.
- Reassess each module against successful VoV, participation, stress-repair, rank, and persistence families before formula specification.

Current conclusion: the prior design should be preserved, narrowed, and re-used only after licensed PIT metadata is accepted. It should not be implemented or converted into candidate formulas now.

## 7. Recommended Program Structure

Recommended structure: several smaller mechanism-specific modules.

Rationale:

- Platform v2 requires one primary mechanism per module.
- The completed OHLCV lifecycle showed that broad scientific spaces create contamination and horizon-shopping pressure.
- Peer-relative research is valuable only if each mechanism can be isolated from existing validated or parked families.
- Licensed PIT metadata must be accepted before real candidate specification, so the current work should prioritize scientific sequencing rather than allocation.

Ranked future module queue after the source gate clears:

1. Peer-Relative Post-Stress Repair and Stabilization Asymmetry Module
2. Peer-Group Rank Persistence After Drawdown Module
3. Industry-Relative Liquidity Stabilization Module
4. Sector-Relative Volatility Normalization Module
5. Cross-Peer Dispersion Compression and Re-Expansion Module
6. Economically Conditioned Leadership Recovery Module

Recommended first post-license research module:

Peer-Relative Post-Stress Repair and Stabilization Asymmetry Module

This module is first because it has the clearest scientific bridge from successful stress-repair and participation evidence, the strongest potential to distinguish idiosyncratic repair from broad recovery, and the most direct contamination questions to freeze before formula specification.

## 13. Platform v2 Lessons Recorded

This frontier should carry forward the following institutional lessons:

- Hypothesis-first discipline must precede candidate allocation.
- Orthogonality must be reviewed before formulas exist.
- Contamination controls must be frozen before empirical discovery.
- Primary horizons must be frozen and cannot be rescued by unrelated short-horizon behavior.
- Bounded refinement is a guardrail, not a license to search broadly.
- Reproducibility depends on immutable lineage and checksum-based artifact integrity.
- Negative evidence is a permanent research asset, not a reason to reopen parked modules.
- PIT metadata must be licensed, historical, and auditable before peer-relative signals can be scientifically interpreted.
