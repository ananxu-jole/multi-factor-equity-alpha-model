## 2. Scientific Definition Of ML Readiness

ML readiness means Project Underdog has enough validated, independent, well-understood, time-safe, reproducible, and governed information for a learning system to plausibly learn genuine structure rather than artifacts.

ML readiness depends on:

| Requirement | Scientific reason |
|---|---|
| Information quality | Inputs must represent real, time-safe evidence rather than leakage, stale metadata, or source artifacts. |
| Information diversity | A learning layer needs multiple independent mechanisms; otherwise it will mostly reweight one dominant family. |
| Scientific understanding | Surviving and failed mechanisms must be understood enough to prevent opaque resurrection of rejected ideas. |
| Governance | Lifecycle rules must control candidate proliferation, artifact drift, and post hoc rescue. |
| Contamination control | ML can amplify hidden leakage or redundancy faster than manual research. |
| Validation maturity | Frozen horizons, validation separation, and negative-evidence rules must exist before flexible learners are considered. |
| Reproducibility | Inputs, labels, artifacts, and decisions must be reconstructable and auditable. |

Dataset size is not enough. A large dataset of redundant, contaminated, or poorly understood features would increase confidence in noise, not scientific knowledge.

## 5. Scientific Understanding Assessment

Project Underdog understands its current evidence well enough to avoid naive ML, but not well enough to justify ML.

What is currently understood:

- surviving evidence is strongest around repair/stabilization, participation, volatility structure, persistence/rank, and VoV;
- rejected mechanisms often failed because of horizon weakness, redundancy, poor OHLCV measurability, contamination, or overabstract narratives;
- contamination pathways include own-feature duplication, market-state duplication, family duplication, current metadata, identity leakage, survivorship, source revision leakage, and horizon rescue;
- redundancy is a major risk around repair, volatility compression, VoV, persistence/rank, transition timing, and leadership language;
- contextual interpretation may be valuable without becoming alpha;
- falsification, retirement, reopening, and anti-resurrection logic are explicitly defined.

Why this matters:

ML would make it easier to rediscover the same families under opaque combinations. Scientific understanding must precede ML so that learned behavior can be interpreted against existing mechanisms and negative evidence.

## 11. Risks Of Premature ML

Premature ML would create several scientific risks:

- learning contamination from current-state metadata, future-known context, source revisions, or survivor-only universes;
- amplifying redundancy among repair, volatility, persistence/rank, and transition families;
- overfitting a narrow set of related mechanisms;
- hidden leakage through identity, peer membership, delisting, and context construction;
- rediscovering existing mechanisms under opaque combinations;
- unstable interpretation across regimes or horizons;
- false confidence from large sample size or many features;
- governance violations through untracked feature proliferation;
- resurrection of parked mechanisms through transformed inputs;
- accidental promotion of diagnostic or explanatory information into predictive use.

The central danger is not that ML would fail noisily. The sharper danger is that it could look sophisticated while learning artifacts Project Underdog already knows how to avoid.

## 12. Conditions Required Before ML

Before ML may begin scientifically, Project Underdog should have:

- a broader inventory of validated or strongly supported independent information families;
- accepted authority evidence for any external inputs;
- PIT identity and lineage evidence;
- PIT economic-context evidence if context is used;
- clear separation between alpha, context, explanatory, diagnostic, governance, and negative information;
- contamination controls across own-security, market, family, identity, source, and peer-definition pathways;
- frozen horizon and validation separation adapted to any future ML lifecycle;
- reproducibility, artifact-lineage, and audit requirements for all inputs and outputs;
- preserved negative-evidence constraints preventing learned resurrection;
- evidence that ML would combine independent information rather than reweight one dominant family;
- explicit scientific stopping and retirement rules.

These are scientific conditions only. They do not define implementation.

## 13. Expected Role Of ML

If ML eventually becomes justified, its scientific role should be to:

- combine independent alpha families;
- learn nonlinear interactions among already valid information sources;
- condition weights on validated context;
- estimate uncertainty across regimes or information states;
- adapt to predeclared regimes without post hoc rescue;
- discover interactions that remain interpretable against the scientific inventory.

ML should not replace:

- source authority;
- PIT identity and lineage;
- economic-context validity;
- hypothesis discipline;
- formula-readiness gates;
- contamination review;
- negative-evidence preservation;
- validation separation;
- reproducibility and auditability;
- governance decisions;
- scientific interpretation.

ML should be a later research layer, not a substitute for missing science.

## 14. Recommended Scientific Disposition

Recommended disposition: `CONTINUE_PHASE5_WITHOUT_ML`

Reasoning:

- WS8 identifies the primary bottleneck as absent external authority evidence.
- The information inventory remains concentrated around repair/stabilization, participation, volatility, VoV, persistence, rank, transition, and market state.
- External context is conceptual, not authoritative or implemented.
- No peer-relative empirical evidence exists.
- No external-information formulas, candidates, panels, IC, validation, or implementation exist.
- Parked mechanisms remain parked and must not be resurrected through flexible learning.
- Platform v2 governance supports continued disciplined science, not premature ML.

The current evidence is strong enough to keep Phase 5 moving, but not strong enough to introduce ML.

## 15. Relationship To Future Implementation

The appropriate future sequence is scientific, then design, then implementation:

1. Complete Phase 5 scientific consistency and terminology harmonization.
2. Resolve or explicitly bound authority, identity, and context gaps.
3. Conduct bounded formula-readiness reviews only for modules that satisfy prerequisites.
4. Define formulas and panels only under separate authorization.
5. Run empirical discovery and validation only under frozen, governed protocols.
6. Rebuild the integrated information inventory after external evidence exists.
7. Reassess ML readiness only if validated independent information diversity improves.
8. Consider ML only as a separately governed research layer.

This note authorizes none of those later implementation steps.
