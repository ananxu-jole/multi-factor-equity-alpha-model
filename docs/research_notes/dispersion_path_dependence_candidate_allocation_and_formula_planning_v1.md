## SECTION 6 - Scientific Expectation Freeze

The following planned candidate families are scientific planning names only. They are not executable candidate IDs.

### 6.1 Relapse Resilience After Temporary Calm

Mechanism:

- Disagreement Relapse Resilience.

Scientific question:

- Do securities resilient during renewed disagreement after temporary calm behave differently from securities that only appeared strong during calm?

Expected primary horizon:

- h5/h10.

Expected secondary horizon:

- h20 as durability evidence only.

Expected activation frequency:

- Episodic but not rare.
- Should activate around relapse-after-calm paths, not on all rising-dispersion dates.

Expected coverage:

- Broad enough on active dates for cross-sectional IC interpretation.
- Should not depend on a small event subset or a single crisis window.

Expected turnover expectation:

- Medium.
- Higher than smooth normalization concepts, but not so high that it behaves like event reversal or h1 noise.

Expected orthogonality:

- Should differ from static dispersion because it requires a prior calm or partial normalization state.
- Should differ from persistence because resilience is conditional on disagreement relapse, not prior rank strength alone.
- Should differ from stress repair because relapse need not be a panic recovery state.

Expected contamination risks:

- Rank persistence.
- Rank coherence.
- Hostile/stress repair.
- Static dispersion acceleration.

Expected failure modes:

- Prior winners explain the effect.
- Active dates are too sparse.
- Evidence appears only in crisis windows.
- Simple rising dispersion explains the same behavior.

Expected strongest evidence:

- Positive h5/h10 mean IC with supporting IC IR and positive IC rate.
- Stable active coverage across multiple relapse episodes.
- Incremental evidence over static dispersion and persistence controls.

Expected weakest evidence:

- h1-only strength.
- h20-only rescue.
- High correlation or co-activation with persistence, rank coherence, or stress repair.

### 6.2 Disagreement Path Divergence From Volatility/Stress

Mechanism:

- Disagreement Path Divergence.

Scientific question:

- Does the path of cross-sectional disagreement add information when it diverges from volatility, VoV, or stress-state paths?

Expected primary horizon:

- h5/h10.

Expected secondary horizon:

- h20 as durability evidence only.

Expected activation frequency:

- Moderate to low.
- Should activate only when disagreement path and volatility/stress paths meaningfully differ.

Expected coverage:

- Moderate on active dates.
- Coverage should not collapse to a handful of securities or dates.

Expected turnover expectation:

- Medium-low to medium.
- Path divergence should not require daily flipping.

Expected orthogonality:

- Highest expected orthogonality within the module.
- Should directly test incremental information beyond VoV, volatility compression, stress repair, and static dispersion.

Expected contamination risks:

- VoV.
- Volatility compression.
- Hostile/stress repair.
- Indicator engineering without economic meaning.

Expected failure modes:

- VoV or volatility compression explains most behavior.
- Divergence is too rare for stable interpretation.
- The future formula becomes a mechanical difference between indicators rather than an economic path concept.

Expected strongest evidence:

- Positive h5/h10 evidence when disagreement path is distinct from volatility/stress path.
- Low or moderate contamination correlation versus VoV and volatility compression references.
- Strong anchor delta versus static dispersion.

Expected weakest evidence:

- Only works when volatility is calming.
- Only works in stress-repair windows.
- No clear active-state interpretation.

### 6.3 Elevated Disagreement Stabilization

Mechanism:

- Elevated Disagreement Stabilization.

Scientific question:

- Do securities that remain orderly while market disagreement is elevated but stabilizing carry positive forward information?

Expected primary horizon:

- h5/h10.

Expected secondary horizon:

- h20 as durability evidence only.

Expected activation frequency:

- Moderate.
- Should activate in elevated-but-stabilizing disagreement paths, not in calm states.

Expected coverage:

- Broad enough for cross-sectional comparison during elevated disagreement.
- Should not be limited to stress events only.

Expected turnover expectation:

- Medium-low.
- Stabilization should imply less churn than relapse concepts.

Expected orthogonality:

- Should differ from static dispersion by requiring stabilization after elevated disagreement.
- Should differ from volatility compression because the primary state is cross-sectional disagreement, not volatility decline.

Expected contamination risks:

- Volatility compression.
- Hostile/stress repair.
- VoV calming.
- Rank coherence.

Expected failure modes:

- Evidence disappears after volatility-compression controls.
- Evidence exists only after drawdown/stress repair.
- Stable ranks or low churn explain the signal.

Expected strongest evidence:

- Positive h5/h10 evidence in elevated-but-stabilizing disagreement states.
- Clear incremental evidence beyond static elevated dispersion and volatility compression.

Expected weakest evidence:

- h20-only result.
- One-window dominance.
- High overlap with volatility compression after stress stabilization.

### 6.4 Consensus Formation Without Crowding

Mechanism:

- Consensus Formation Without Crowding.

Scientific question:

- Does orderly disagreement normalization identify securities benefiting from delayed consensus formation without rewarding crowded leadership?

Expected primary horizon:

- h5/h10.

Expected secondary horizon:

- h20 as durability evidence only.

Expected activation frequency:

- Moderate.
- Should activate during normalization paths after prior disagreement.

Expected coverage:

- Broad enough to avoid leadership-only selection.
- Should not shrink to a narrow group of mature winners.

Expected turnover expectation:

- Low to medium.
- Smooth consensus should not behave like event reversal or daily rank flipping.

Expected orthogonality:

- Should differ from parked non-hostile transition by avoiding leadership-continuation claims.
- Should differ from rank coherence and persistence by making disagreement normalization the primary condition.

Expected contamination risks:

- Parked non-hostile transition.
- Momentum.
- Rank coherence.
- Persistence.
- Leadership crowding.

Expected failure modes:

- Mature leadership or prior winners explain the signal.
- Normalization is merely calm-market momentum.
- Evidence resembles the parked non-hostile transition family.

Expected strongest evidence:

- Positive h5/h10 evidence during normalization after disagreement.
- Demonstrated separation from leadership continuation, rank coherence, and persistence references.

Expected weakest evidence:

- Strongest performance in h20 only.
- High overlap with low-extension leadership or non-hostile transition semantics.
- No incremental value beyond rank stability.

### 6.5 Deferred: Smooth Versus Burst Resolution

Mechanism:

- Smooth Versus Burst Resolution.

Allocation:

- No first-batch candidate family.

Reason for deferral:

- The mechanism is scientifically interesting but carries the highest first-batch contamination risk. It can easily become event clustering, volume shock reversal, plain reversal, or stress repair unless the formula specification has stronger event-control language than currently available.

Future expansion condition:

- May be reconsidered only after first-batch research review or a separately approved diagnostic design note.

Expected primary horizon if later approved:

- h5 primary, h10 secondary.

Expected strongest future evidence:

- Evidence that smooth disagreement resolution differs from burst-like resolution after controlling for volume shock reversal and plain reversal.

Expected failure mode:

- Behavior is fully explained by clustered volume events or one-day reversal effects.

## SECTION 7 - Priority Ranking

Highest priority:

- Relapse Resilience After Temporary Calm.
- Disagreement Path Divergence From Volatility/Stress.

Rationale:

These two planned candidate families best satisfy the Platform v2 demand for path dependence and orthogonality. Relapse cannot be defined without sequence. Divergence directly tests incremental information beyond VoV, volatility compression, stress repair, and static dispersion.

High priority:

- Elevated Disagreement Stabilization.
- Consensus Formation Without Crowding.

Rationale:

Both are economically plausible and scientifically useful, but each needs stronger contamination controls. Stabilization can drift into volatility compression or stress repair. Consensus can drift into momentum, rank coherence, persistence, or parked non-hostile transition.

Medium priority:

- None in the first batch.

Rationale:

The first batch should avoid filling the middle of the budget with lower-conviction variants. Unused capacity is preferable to a candidate that exists mainly because six slots are available.

Future expansion:

- Smooth Versus Burst Resolution.

Rationale:

This concept has diagnostic value, but first-batch inclusion would increase the risk that Dispersion Path-Dependence becomes volume shock reversal or event clustering under a new name.

## SECTION 10 - Expected Scientific Outcomes

Success would mean:

- at least one planned candidate family produces positive h5/h10 evidence in the predeclared direction;
- evidence is supported by IC IR and positive IC rate, not mean IC alone;
- active coverage is sufficient for cross-sectional interpretation;
- results are not dominated by one window or crisis episode;
- strongest evidence survives static dispersion and prior dispersion anchor comparison;
- contamination references do not explain the core behavior;
- the module teaches something distinct about path, memory, transition, relapse, normalization, or divergence of disagreement.

Stronger family-level success would require:

- coherent h5/h10 evidence from more than one planned candidate family;
- evidence across at least two mechanism types, preferably one relapse or divergence candidate plus one stabilization or consensus candidate;
- no sibling-duplicate pattern;
- contamination controls supporting the claim that Dispersion Path-Dependence is not merely VoV, volatility compression, stress repair, rank coherence, persistence, or static dispersion.

Valuable learning even if parked:

- whether prior dispersion weakness reflects the wrong horizon, weak mechanism, or true lack of alpha content;
- whether h5/h10 is the only plausible dispersion-path horizon;
- whether rank coherence and persistence already capture useful orderly cross-sectional structure;
- whether volatility compression and stress repair explain most elevated-disagreement behavior;
- whether static dispersion anchors are sufficient and path terms add little;
- whether future dispersion work requires point-in-time sector, industry, flow, or event metadata;
- whether Smooth Versus Burst Resolution should remain separate from dispersion path-dependence.
