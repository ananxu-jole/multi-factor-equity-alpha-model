## 2. Scientific Meaning Of Contamination

Contamination is any condition that makes a scientific claim appear to contain independent information when it is actually caused by leakage, invalid construction, duplicated information, unsupported context, or post hoc interpretation.

Contamination classes:

| Class | Scientific meaning | Peer-relative risk |
|---|---|---|
| Temporal contamination | Evidence uses information unavailable at the historical decision date. | Future classifications, future peer membership, future delistings, or post-event knowledge define the peer comparison. |
| Identity contamination | The security, issuer, company, listing, ticker, or share class is misidentified. | Repair is measured on one entity while peers or history belong to another. |
| Peer-membership contamination | Peer sets are not PIT-valid, economically comparable, or stable enough for the role. | Apparent alpha is manufactured by bad peers. |
| Source contamination | Source definitions, revisions, coverage, or date semantics inject hidden bias. | Composite external evidence is not scientifically reconstructable. |
| Survivorship contamination | Failed, acquired, delisted, or missing securities are absent. | Peer averages look healthier and relative repair is overstated. |
| Delisting contamination | Terminal states are missing or mishandled. | Failure-to-recover disappears from peer comparisons. |
| Classification contamination | Current or retrospective sector/industry labels are used historically. | Future-known economic context creates look-ahead. |
| Corporate-event contamination | Mergers, spinoffs, reorganizations, or bankruptcies create false continuity. | Post-event recovery is attributed to the wrong predecessor or peer group. |
| Market-state contamination | Broad market or regime effects are mislabeled as peer effects. | Common beta recovery becomes "peer-relative repair." |
| Own-feature contamination | The peer-relative measure is a rescaled own-security OHLCV feature. | No new information exists beyond own repair, reversal, volatility, or rank. |
| Target contamination | Future return or outcome information influences feature, peer, or horizon choice. | The module is optimized around the answer. |
| Validation contamination | Validation uses unapproved inputs, repeated tuning, or post hoc thresholds. | Apparent robustness is an artifact of process leakage. |
| Selection contamination | The module survives because it was selected after seeing related evidence. | Positive results reflect research path dependence rather than mechanism. |
| Interpretation contamination | A result is explained after the fact using whichever context fits. | False novelty is created by narrative flexibility. |

Contamination can create apparently strong peer-relative evidence even when no independent information exists because peer construction can import future knowledge, suppress failed cases, repackage known OHLCV states, or use unstable context to make ordinary recovery appear idiosyncratic.

## 3. Scientific Meaning Of Orthogonality

Orthogonality means scientific distinctness and incremental knowledge, not merely low measured correlation.

Distinctions:

- Statistical low correlation: a numerical relationship may be low, but the mechanism can still duplicate an existing family under different activation dates.
- Conditional independence: a relationship may remain after a future control comparison, but that does not prove economic distinctness.
- Mechanism independence: the causal or behavioral story differs from existing families.
- Contextual independence: the module depends on peer context in a way that own-security and market state cannot reproduce.
- Informational incrementality: peer-relative information changes what the project can know beyond current evidence.
- Residual novelty: information remains after reasonable conceptual comparisons to existing families, without defining a residualization procedure here.
- Economic distinctness: peer comparison has a valid economic interpretation rather than a statistical normalization.
- Validation independence: any future result must survive separate validation and not inherit credibility from related OHLCV families.

Low correlation alone is insufficient. A signal can have low correlation because of sparse activation, different scaling, noisy construction, or invalid peers while still being the same scientific idea. Orthogonality requires mechanism clarity, time safety, peer dependence, family-level separation, and falsifiable incremental claims.

## 4. Selected-Module Reconstruction

Selected module: `Peer-Relative Post-Stress Repair And Stabilization Asymmetry`

Conceptual reconstruction:

- Core scientific mechanism: after common stress, a security's repair, stabilization, or failure-to-recover relative to valid economic peers may reveal idiosyncratic strength or weakness not visible in own-security behavior alone.
- Intended peer comparator: securities or economic companies that were valid, active, PIT-known, economically comparable peers at the stress and observation dates.
- Stress context: a common hostile, stressed, drawdown, volatility, liquidity, or peer-wide dislocation state defined without future knowledge.
- Repair context: recovery from stress in price, participation, liquidity, volatility, rank, or transition state, without formula definitions here.
- Stabilization context: reduction of disorder or instability after stress, distinct only if it is not merely volatility compression.
- Positive asymmetry: faster or stronger repair/stabilization than peers.
- Negative asymmetry: lagging repair, failure-to-recover, or deterioration while peers recover.
- Expected information contribution: separation of common recovery from security-specific repair or fragility.
- Expected relationship to existing families: closest to hostile/stress repair, participation repair, volatility compression, persistence/rank, and transition-state work.
- Principal scientific uncertainty: whether the peer-relative comparator adds true information or merely relabels own-security repair, market state, existing repair families, or invalid peer construction.

No formulas are defined.

## 6. Own-Security Contamination

A peer-relative repair feature can merely restate own-security behavior if the peer comparator adds no scientific information.

Own-feature overlap channels:

- own drawdown: the module selects damaged securities rather than peer-relative repair;
- own recovery speed: relative repair mirrors own rebound velocity;
- own return reversal: repair is short-term reversal;
- own volatility normalization: stabilization is declining own volatility;
- own volume recovery: repair is volume rebound;
- own liquidity recovery: relative improvement is liquidity normalization;
- own persistence: repaired names simply persist;
- own rank change: peer-relative state is broad rank recovery;
- own transition state: peer-relative behavior is entry into an existing repair state.

Conceptual evidence required:

- the peer comparator changes interpretation relative to own-security repair;
- own-security anchors remain necessary but insufficient;
- peer context identifies common versus idiosyncratic repair;
- negative peer-relative states behave differently from own weakness;
- future evidence can show that peer ablation changes scientific interpretation.

## 7. Market-State Contamination

Market-state contamination occurs when broad market or regime behavior is mislabeled as peer-relative information.

Risks:

- common market stress affects most securities;
- common recovery lifts peers and target security together;
- beta effects create apparent relative repair;
- market-wide volatility normalization creates stabilization;
- market participation recovery creates peer-wide repair;
- sector-wide rebound can look like company-specific improvement;
- synchronized transition states make early or late repair hard to interpret.

Apparent peer-relative outperformance may merely reflect market or sector exposure if the peer set is more cyclical, more volatile, smaller, less liquid, or differently beta-exposed than the target security. A future module must conceptually separate economic-peer context from broad market recovery before any empirical work could be meaningful.

## 8. Existing Repair-Family Contamination

Peer-relative repair can represent several different scientific roles:

- new mechanism: peer context reveals idiosyncratic repair not in own OHLCV;
- conditioning layer: peer context changes when existing repair matters;
- benchmark transformation: peer repair is only a comparison scale;
- refinement: peer context narrows hostile/stress repair;
- residual: peer context removes common recovery but does not define a new family;
- disguised duplicate: peer-relative repair is the hostile/stress repair family in different language.

Evidence that would later distinguish these interpretations:

- predeclared mechanism separation from own repair;
- clear peer dependence;
- comparison to hostile/stress-repair references;
- survival of interpretation under peer ablations;
- negative controls that fail as expected;
- no reliance on static/current peer labels.

## 9. Participation And Liquidity Contamination

Participation and liquidity are major contamination risks because existing Project Underdog evidence already contains participation and liquidity repair.

Overlap channels:

- participation breadth recovery;
- volume repair;
- liquidity state shifts;
- trading-intensity normalization;
- peer-wide liquidity recovery;
- liquidity scale differences inside peer sets.

Relative repair may be driven entirely by liquidity differences if the target's volume or tradability restores faster than peers. That may be useful, but it is not a new peer-relative economic mechanism unless the peer comparator contributes distinct context beyond participation and liquidity references.

## 10. Volatility-Compression Contamination

Stabilization can collapse into volatility compression.

Overlap channels:

- faster volatility normalization;
- lower residual volatility;
- peer-wide stabilization;
- idiosyncratic stabilization;
- apparent repair created by declining volatility.

Repair and stabilization are scientifically distinct only when repair captures recovery of state or behavior not reducible to volatility calming. They are the same mechanism under different language when stabilization means only lower volatility after stress.

## 11. VoV Contamination

VoV contamination is unresolved and important.

Overlap channels:

- VoV elevation before repair;
- VoV normalization after stress;
- peer-wide VoV shock;
- idiosyncratic VoV shock;
- persistence of VoV states.

Existing VoV evidence is meaningful but still carries contamination-review caveats. A peer-relative repair module must not treat VoV as already fully independent, and it must not import peer-relative VoV concepts into the first module unless future Workstream 5/8 evidence supports doing so.

## 12. Persistence And Rank Contamination

Repair asymmetry can collapse into persistence or rank behavior.

Overlap channels:

- rank persistence after drawdown;
- rank recovery;
- peer-relative leadership;
- rank churn;
- stable relative weakness;
- broad cross-sectional rank transformation.

The module becomes a rank or persistence family if "repair relative to peers" is simply a rank improvement, leadership retention, or low rank churn. Peer-relative rank may later be useful, but it must prove that economic-peer rank differs scientifically from existing rank and persistence evidence.

## 13. Transition-State Contamination

Transition-state contamination occurs when peer-relative repair is just transition timing.

Overlap channels:

- entering repair before peers;
- exiting stress before peers;
- normalization timing;
- unresolved stress;
- transition ordering;
- state divergence.

A genuine peer-relative repair mechanism should say more than "this security entered repair earlier." If transition order is the mechanism, it should be treated as a transition-state hypothesis or contextual reinterpretation, not as independent repair unless peer context adds distinct economic meaning.

## 14. Peer-Definition Contamination

Invalid peers can manufacture apparent alpha.

Peer-definition risks:

- retrospective classification;
- current classifications used historically;
- unstable peer membership;
- taxonomy changes;
- fallback hierarchy changes;
- too few peers;
- dominant peers;
- duplicate share classes;
- size mismatch;
- identity mismatch;
- corporate-event mismatch;
- future-known peer eligibility.

Invalid peers can make a security look strong by comparing it to the wrong group, excluding failed peers, using a future taxonomy, or collapsing economically distinct entities. Peer construction remains blocked until authority, identity, and context frameworks are satisfied.

## 15. Identity And Lineage Contamination

Identity errors propagate directly into peer-relative measurements.

Risks:

- ticker reuse maps different companies into one history;
- ticker changes break or falsely continue identity;
- mergers create ambiguous predecessor/successor continuity;
- spinoffs split economic history;
- relistings blur terminal and new histories;
- multiple share classes duplicate economic exposure;
- ADRs and ordinary shares mix distinct securities with related companies;
- company-security remapping changes peer context;
- missing delistings remove negative outcomes;
- terminal-state errors distort recovery and deterioration.

If identity is wrong, own behavior, peer membership, classification, delisting status, and return history can all be wrong. WS2 therefore remains a prerequisite for any future empirical peer-relative work.

## 16. Temporal Contamination

Time safety is required for:

- stress identification;
- peer membership;
- classifications;
- size context;
- corporate events;
- listing status;
- delisting status;
- source-known dates;
- observation windows;
- forecast horizons;
- refinement decisions.

Frozen-horizon discipline is preserved. A future module must not select stress dates, peer membership, contexts, asymmetry direction, or horizons based on future outcomes. This note defines no executable timing logic.

## 17. Source Contamination

External sources can be individually credible yet jointly contaminated.

Risk dimensions:

- identity authority differs from classification authority;
- date semantics differ by source;
- revision behavior differs by table or record type;
- coverage differs through time;
- missingness is source-specific;
- retention may prevent reconstruction;
- source conflict may be unresolved.

Combining sources can create contaminated composite evidence if one source supplies PIT identity, another supplies current classifications, and a third supplies revised event history without aligned known-date semantics. Role-specific authority is therefore mandatory.

## 18. Survivorship And Delisting Contamination

Missing or mishandled terminal securities can distort:

- peer-set composition;
- average peer repair;
- relative rank;
- recovery comparison;
- deterioration comparison;
- negative-evidence interpretation.

Conceptual fail-closed conditions:

- delisted securities are missing from historical peer sets;
- delisting dates are unknown where active eligibility matters;
- terminal states conflict across sources;
- failed peers are excluded from recovery baselines;
- missing final records make failure-to-recover invisible.

## 19. Corporate-Event Contamination

Corporate events can create false repair or stabilization.

Event risks:

- mergers and acquisitions can replace one economic entity with another;
- spinoffs can create new peer context;
- reorganizations can break continuity;
- recapitalizations can alter security economics;
- bankruptcies can transform terminal-state interpretation.

Post-event observations should be:

- comparable when identity, economics, and peer context remain continuous;
- conditionally comparable when continuity is plausible but event impact must be governed;
- quarantined when continuity is ambiguous;
- excluded from future empirical use when identity, event timing, or comparability is unresolved.

No exclusion windows are defined.

## 20. Asymmetry Contamination

Asymmetry is central to the selected module, but it can contaminate the science if defined after outcomes are known.

Asymmetry pairs:

- faster repair versus slower repair;
- stabilization versus deterioration;
- participation versus non-participation;
- temporary divergence versus persistent divergence;
- positive versus negative rank movement.

Asymmetry adds scientific meaning when positive and negative divergence have different mechanisms or consequences. It introduces post hoc fragmentation when direction, persistence, or context is chosen after observing results.

## 21. Context-Fragmentation Risk

The module could fragment across:

- market regimes;
- stress states;
- peer states;
- size states;
- sector states;
- volatility states;
- participation states;
- horizons.

Excessive conditioning creates false novelty by letting a weak mechanism survive only in narrow, retrospectively selected contexts. It also harms reproducibility because future samples may not contain enough comparable states. Context must be justified by mechanism, not performance.

## 22. False Novelty Taxonomy

| False novelty form | Mechanism | Detection concept | Scientific consequence | Required response |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Renamed existing feature | Existing repair or stabilization receives a peer label. | Mechanism matches existing family. | No new family. | Reclassify as existing-family refinement or reject. |
| Peer-demeaned existing feature | Own feature minus peer average. | Peer comparator adds scale but not meaning. | Benchmark transformation only. | Treat as contextual comparison. |
| Conditional version of existing feature | Existing family active only under peer context. | Context changes activation but not mechanism. | Conditioning layer. | Require distinct hypothesis or refinement classification. |
| Rank transformation | Peer-relative state is rank movement. | Broad rank explains concept. | Rank-family overlap. | Compare to rank/persistence references. |
| Residualized duplicate | Remaining value is claimed after adjustment without mechanism. | No economic interpretation. | Statistical artifact risk. | Require mechanism definition. |
| Horizon-shifted duplicate | Similar feature at different horizon. | Mechanism unchanged. | Horizon shopping risk. | Preserve frozen horizons; reject if post hoc. |
| Sign-flipped duplicate | Negative side of existing feature is claimed new. | Direction chosen after results. | Interpretation contamination. | Predeclare asymmetry or reject. |
| Context-fragmented duplicate | Narrow regimes rescue old mechanism. | Many conditional slices. | Weak reproducibility. | Fail closed if fragmentation is excessive. |
| Source-induced artifact | Source coverage or revisions create pattern. | Pattern aligns with source missingness/revision. | Source contamination. | Require source-role authority or reject. |
| Identity-induced artifact | Ticker/entity errors create false continuity. | Pattern disappears under accepted identity. | Identity contamination. | Quarantine or reject. |
