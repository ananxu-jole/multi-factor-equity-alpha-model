## 2. Scientific Meaning Of Falsification

Falsification means evidence strong enough to reject, retire, narrow, reinterpret, quarantine, or preserve as negative evidence a scientific claim. It is not merely a failed run or inconvenient result.

Distinctions:

| Type | Meaning | Scientific consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Hypothesis falsification | The specific claim does not survive its predeclared tests or controls. | Retire, reject, or reinterpret the hypothesis. |
| Mechanism falsification | The proposed behavioral or economic mechanism is contradicted. | Retire the mechanism or require a genuinely new mechanism. |
| Implementation failure | Code, artifacts, schema, or execution fails. | Fix implementation or rerun only if the scientific design remains intact. |
| Source failure | External evidence is unavailable, unauthorized, temporally inadequate, or not reproducible. | Hold, diagnostic-only, redesign, or reject measurability; not automatically mechanism falsification. |
| Identity failure | Security, issuer, listing, ticker, or company identity is invalid. | Quarantine or reject affected claims; mechanism may remain untested. |
| Peer-definition failure | No valid peer comparator exists for the claim. | Reinterpret, hold, or reject the peer-relative hypothesis. |
| Contamination invalidation | Leakage or invalid construction explains the evidence. | Reject contaminated result; possibly reject hypothesis if contamination is design-level. |
| Redundancy rejection | Existing families already explain the proposed information. | Reject as independent family; maybe preserve as refinement/control. |
| Horizon failure | Predeclared horizon logic fails. | Retire or narrow; do not rescue through horizon shopping. |
| Validation failure | Future validation contradicts discovery or design evidence. | Freeze, retire, or preserve as negative validation evidence. |
| Inconclusive evidence | Evidence cannot support a decision. | Hold or refine evidence requirements without claiming survival. |

A hypothesis can fail scientifically even if an implementation functions. A technically correct peer-relative transform may still be redundant, contaminated, unfalsifiable, sourced from invalid peers, or inconsistent with frozen horizon and negative-control expectations.

## 3. Scientific Meaning Of Negative Evidence

Negative evidence is preserved information showing that a hypothesis, mechanism, context, source role, peer definition, horizon, direction, implementation path, or interpretation did not support the intended scientific claim under documented conditions.

It differs from:

- no result: no adequate test or interpretable evidence exists;
- missing data: evidence could not be generated;
- insufficient power: the test was too weak to decide;
- implementation defects: technical failure prevents scientific interpretation;
- source unavailability: evidence source does not exist or cannot be used;
- contamination: evidence is invalidated by leakage or construction failure;
- weak evidence: result is positive or mixed but not persuasive;
- conflicting evidence: evidence points in incompatible directions;
- null evidence: clean absence of support;
- adverse evidence: evidence contradicts direction or mechanism;
- evidence of redundancy: information exists but is already captured elsewhere.

Negative evidence is a permanent scientific asset because it prevents repeated mining of weak ideas, preserves what was learned, strengthens future falsification standards, and protects the project from aesthetic relabeling of failed mechanisms.

## 5. Falsification Target Hierarchy

Targets of falsification:

- individual transformation;
- individual candidate;
- hypothesis variant;
- module;
- mechanism family;
- contextual interpretation;
- source role;
- peer-definition approach;
- broader scientific claim.

Failure at one level does not automatically falsify every other level. A bad transformation may not falsify a mechanism. A source-role failure may not falsify a hypothesis. A clean module-level failure may not retire a broader family if the module tested only one narrow variant. Conversely, a mechanism-level contradiction can retire many transformations at once.

## 8. Null-Result Science

Null results are not all equal.

| Null type | Meaning | Disposition logic |
|---|---|---|
| Scientifically clean null | Adequate evidence shows no support under valid design. | Retire claim or module depending on scope. |
| Underpowered null | Evidence is too thin or sparse. | Inconclusive or hold; do not claim survival. |
| Contaminated null | Test cannot be trusted because construction is invalid. | Quarantine result; fix contamination only if design remains valid. |
| Source-limited null | Source coverage or authority is insufficient. | Hold or diagnostic-only; not mechanism falsification unless measurability is impossible. |
| Context-specific null | Claim fails in a predeclared context. | Retire that context or narrow with justification. |
| Horizon-specific null | Claim fails at a predeclared horizon. | Retire horizon claim; do not rescue post hoc. |
| Conflicting null | Some evidence supports and some contradicts. | Preserve conflict; require bounded review. |
| Irreproducible null | Null cannot be reconstructed. | Not authoritative negative evidence; preserve as warning. |

A clean null should retire a hypothesis when it directly tests the hypothesis under valid authority, identity, context, and contamination controls. Nulls should remain inconclusive when evidence quality, power, source authority, identity, or context is inadequate.

## 9. Redundancy-Driven Rejection

A hypothesis should be rejected as redundant when it adds no new scientific information.

Redundancy channels:

- high conceptual overlap;
- equivalent mechanism;
- equivalent temporal signature;
- equivalent context dependence;
- equivalent validation behavior;
- peer-demeaned duplication;
- rank-transformed duplication;
- residualized duplication;
- sign-flipped duplication;
- renamed duplication.

A statistically different output can still be scientifically redundant if it measures the same state, relies on the same mechanism, activates in the same contexts, and fails to change interpretation beyond scaling, ranking, lagging, or normalization.

## 10. Contamination-Driven Rejection

Contamination invalidates the hypothesis rather than merely one implementation when the contamination is inseparable from the claim.

Contamination channels:

- retrospective peer assignment;
- future-known classification;
- identity leakage;
- delisting omissions;
- survivor bias;
- source revision leakage;
- corporate-event leakage;
- current-state metadata;
- horizon selection;
- post hoc asymmetry;
- context fragmentation;
- validation leakage.

Contamination classes:

| Class | Meaning | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Correctable contamination | A narrow artifact or mapping issue can be fixed without changing the hypothesis. | Quarantine and retest only after correction. |
| Implementation-specific contamination | The implementation is invalid but design may remain valid. | Reject implementation; hold hypothesis. |
| Design-level contamination | The design requires contaminated information. | Redesign or reject. |
| Hypothesis-fatal contamination | The hypothesis cannot be stated or measured without leakage. | Reject as contaminated. |

## 11. Source And Authority Failure

Source limitations should be handled precisely.

| Source condition | Meaning | Correct disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Source unavailable | Required evidence is not available. | Hold or redesign. |
| Source unauthorized | Evidence cannot be used under current authority rules. | Hold or diagnostic-only. |
| Source temporally inadequate | Date semantics do not support PIT use. | Diagnostic-only or reject source role. |
| Source historically incomplete | Coverage cannot support intended universe or time span. | Narrow, hold, or reject role. |
| Source irreproducible | Evidence cannot be preserved or reconstructed. | Reject as authoritative evidence. |
| Source role mismatch | Source is authoritative for one role but not another. | Redesign role usage. |
| Source conflict | Credible sources disagree. | Hold, conflict review, or reject affected role. |
| Source evidence insufficient | Documentation or samples are inadequate. | Hold pending evidence. |

Source failure must not be misrepresented as scientific falsification unless the hypothesis is impossible to measure under any scientifically acceptable authority path.

## 12. Peer-Definition Falsification

Peer-definition evidence can falsify or constrain the selected module.

Failure modes:

- no defensible economic peers;
- unstable peer meaning;
- sparse peer groups;
- excessive fallback;
- taxonomy-dependent conclusions;
- size-dependent conclusions;
- duplicate economic exposure;
- dominant-peer effects;
- identity-context mismatch;
- conclusions disappear across reasonable peer definitions.

The mechanism is peer-definition fragile when the interpretation changes materially across reasonable, predeclared peer definitions. It is not robust if it works only under one retrospectively chosen taxonomy, size slice, fallback hierarchy, or peer-count condition.

## 13. Horizon Falsification

Frozen-horizon discipline remains mandatory.

Horizon concerns:

- mechanism-specific horizon logic;
- immediate versus short versus medium response;
- sign reversal across horizons;
- isolated positive horizon;
- broad horizon weakness;
- refinement after horizon observation;
- horizon rescue attempts.

A predeclared horizon can fail without falsifying every possible related mechanism, but it should falsify the specific claim tied to that horizon. A nonprimary horizon cannot rescue a failed primary-horizon mechanism unless a separately scoped new hypothesis is created before further testing.

## 14. Context Falsification

Context dependence can be valid or fatal.

Contexts:

- market regime;
- stress regime;
- peer stress;
- sector stress;
- volatility regime;
- liquidity regime;
- participation regime;
- size context;
- corporate-event context.

Distinctions:

- Scientifically necessary conditioning: context is part of the mechanism before testing.
- Narrow but valid contextual evidence: effect exists only in a justified context.
- Unstable context dependence: context behavior changes without explanation.
- Excessive fragmentation: many narrow slices create false novelty.
- Post hoc rescue: context is chosen after observing results.

## 15. Direction And Asymmetry Falsification

Asymmetry should be evaluated before results, not retrofitted.

Asymmetry states:

- faster repair;
- slower repair;
- stabilization;
- deterioration;
- participation;
- non-participation;
- temporary divergence;
- persistent divergence;
- sign inversion.

Asymmetry is a real mechanism dimension when positive and negative states are expected to differ for a predeclared reason. It is directional slicing when one side is selected after results, when sign flips rescue weak evidence, or when persistence is added only after temporary divergence fails.

## 18. Ablation Failure Logic

Future ablation interpretation:

- No peers: if no-peer own-security baseline is equivalent, peer context is not incremental.
- Broad market only: if market context is equivalent, module is market-state relabeling.
- Sector only: if sector-only works and valid economic peers add nothing, the mechanism may be contextual or sector control rather than peer-specific.
- Size only: if size explains behavior, peer economics may be secondary.
- Valid economic peers: should be necessary for the intended peer-relative claim.
- Randomized peers: success weakens or falsifies peer-specific interpretation.
- Current-state peers: success exposes current-metadata leakage if stronger than PIT peers.
- Temporally invalid peers: success exposes look-ahead.
- Own-security anchors: absorption by anchors indicates own-feature duplication.
- Existing repair controls: absorption indicates repair-family redundancy.
- Participation controls: absorption indicates participation/liquidity confounding.
- Volatility controls: absorption indicates volatility-compression overlap.
- VoV controls: absorption indicates VoV overlap.
- Persistence/rank controls: absorption indicates rank or persistence redundancy.
- Transition-state controls: absorption indicates transition timing.

Ablations support the module only if valid peers add scientific interpretation beyond these alternatives and invalid peers/negative controls fail as expected.

## 21. Anti-Resurrection Framework

Rejected ideas must not return through:

- renaming;
- sign inversion;
- horizon shifting;
- context slicing;
- peer relabeling;
- source substitution;
- benchmark substitution;
- residualization;
- rank transformation;
- narrow subgroup selection;
- candidate re-registration.

Legitimate reopening requires a documented new scientific basis. Aesthetic reformulation, implementation convenience, or a new label is not enough.

## 22. Reopening Standard

Retired or rejected work may be reopened only with:

- genuinely new information source;
- genuinely new mechanism;
- resolved contamination pathway;
- corrected identity or context authority;
- pre-specified new falsifiable claim;
- evidence not available during the original decision;
- explicit comparison with preserved negative evidence;
- clear statement of what remains retired;
- lifecycle approval before any future design.

New naming, a more convenient implementation, or selective attention to a favorable subperiod does not qualify.
