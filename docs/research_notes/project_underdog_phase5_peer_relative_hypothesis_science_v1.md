## 2. Scientific Meaning Of Peer-Relative Information

Peer-relative information means a point-in-time claim that a security's behavior is meaningfully different from the behavior of economically comparable entities that were knowable, eligible, and valid peers at that historical time.

It is not the same as:

- absolute security behavior, which asks what the security did by itself;
- market-relative behavior, which compares against a broad market state rather than an economically comparable group;
- sector-relative returns, unless the sector membership is authoritative, PIT-valid, and scientifically appropriate for the mechanism;
- benchmark-relative returns, because benchmark membership may reflect investability or index methodology rather than economic comparability;
- simple demeaning, which mechanically subtracts a group average without proving the group is meaningful;
- cross-sectional ranking, which may compare unrelated securities;
- return correlation or statistical clustering, which may reflect common beta, liquidity, or regime effects rather than economic similarity;
- residualization, which is a statistical operation rather than a scientific peer claim;
- current industry labels, which are diagnostic-only unless historically accepted;
- generic relative strength, which may be momentum under another name.

Peer-relative information should represent contextual deviation from valid economic peers. Its scientific value depends on whether it separates common peer conditions from security-specific repair, deterioration, persistence, volatility normalization, liquidity behavior, or transition timing. A peer-relative transform that only re-labels own-security OHLCV, market state, or static metadata is not new scientific information.

## 3. Incremental-Information Requirement

A peer-relative mechanism adds information only if it can plausibly explain something not already explained by Project Underdog's existing evidence families.

Required independence references:

- own-security OHLCV behavior;
- broad market state;
- sector or benchmark state where authoritative context later exists;
- participation breadth;
- hostile/stress state;
- repair and stabilization families;
- volatility compression;
- VoV;
- rank persistence and rank coherence;
- transition-state features;
- existing cross-sectional rankings.

Distinctions:

| Category | Scientific meaning | Permitted conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Genuinely incremental information | Peer-relative behavior captures a mechanism not reducible to own OHLCV, market state, or existing families. | Eligible for later design after authority, identity, context, and contamination prerequisites exist. |
| Contextual reinterpretation | Peer context changes how an existing OHLCV state is interpreted, but may not be a separate family. | Eligible as a constrained interpretive module or control, not as automatic alpha novelty. |
| Redundant transformation | The peer-relative form mechanically mirrors an existing family. | Not eligible as a distinct hypothesis. |
| Disguised demeaning | The mechanism is merely group normalization without economic interpretation. | Diagnostic only. |
| False novelty | New labels obscure a previously tested or rejected mechanism. | Rejected or requires foundational redefinition. |

The repository's strategic reassessment and Phase 5 roadmap identify peer-relative context as the largest missing independent axis, but they do not prove incremental alpha. This workstream therefore defines hypotheses to test later, not findings.

## 5. Repair And Stabilization Hypotheses

Peer-relative repair and stabilization are the primary candidate family because they begin from Project Underdog's strongest existing evidence: hostile/stress repair, participation repair, liquidity repair, volatility/stress stabilization, and related transition behavior.

Scientifically plausible hypotheses:

- a security repairs faster than valid peers following common stress;
- a security stabilizes while peers continue deteriorating;
- a security's recovery is stronger relative to peers than its absolute recovery suggests;
- peer-wide recovery distinguishes common beta recovery from idiosyncratic repair;
- lagging repair relative to peers predicts continued weakness;
- repair asymmetry carries more information than absolute repair.

Potential incremental contribution:

- distinguishing common peer recovery from security-specific repair;
- identifying relative failure-to-recover as separate from absolute weakness;
- separating market-wide stress normalization from peer-specific normalization;
- making repair interpretation more economically precise.

Duplication risk:

- own-security hostile/stress repair may already contain most signal;
- participation or liquidity repair may explain apparent peer-relative recovery;
- volatility compression may explain stabilization;
- rank persistence may explain relative ordering.

This family is therefore selected as the highest-value hypothesis family for later design, but it remains conceptual until Workstream 5 contamination science and future authority/context evidence permit stronger claims.

## 6. Relative Deterioration Hypotheses

Relative deterioration concerns negative divergence from valid peers. It is not merely the inverse of repair because deterioration may appear before absolute breakdown, after peer recovery, or during apparently normal market conditions.

Hypotheses include:

- deterioration while peers remain stable;
- failure to participate in peer recovery;
- relative liquidity deterioration;
- relative volatility deterioration;
- rank decay within a valid peer set;
- persistent relative weakness after common stress.

Scientific importance:

- negative relative states may detect idiosyncratic fragility earlier than absolute OHLCV;
- peer recovery creates a clearer counterfactual for non-recovery;
- missing delisting evidence and survivor bias can make deterioration appear weaker than it is, so terminal-state science is central.

Main risks:

- disguised negative momentum;
- delisting or missing-record contamination;
- unsupported peer membership;
- excessive overlap with relative repair asymmetry.

## 7. Relative Persistence And Rank Hypotheses

Peer-relative persistence may add information if rank durability is meaningful within economically comparable entities rather than only across the broad universe.

Possible hypotheses:

- persistence of peer-relative leadership;
- persistence of peer-relative repair;
- persistence of peer-relative weakness;
- rank stability within economic peers;
- peer-relative rank churn;
- decay of relative advantages.

Repository constraints:

- `docs/research_notes/persistence_conditional_validation_review_v1.md` preserves persistence as credible conditional evidence with low artifact-level stress-repair contamination in reviewed artifacts.
- `docs/research_notes/rank_coherence_panel_and_redundancy_review_v1.md` documents important redundancy risks inside rank-coherence concepts.
- `docs/research_notes/project_underdog_strategic_program_reassessment_v1.md` classifies persistence and rank-coherence as meaningful conditional candidate-lineages, not broad family proof.

Scientific stance: peer-relative persistence is plausible but secondary. It must prove that economic-peer rank stability is not merely existing rank persistence, market rank, broad leadership, or post-drawdown repair under a different label.

## 8. Relative VoV Hypotheses

Relative VoV hypotheses ask whether volatility-of-volatility state is firm-specific or contextual relative to peers.

Possible hypotheses:

- VoV elevation relative to peers;
- VoV normalization relative to peers;
- peer-wide versus idiosyncratic VoV shocks;
- relative VoV persistence;
- peer-relative VoV as a distinction between firm-specific instability and contextual instability.

Repository constraints:

- `docs/research_notes/ohlcv_volatility_of_volatility_validation_results_review_and_candidate_registration_recommendation_v1.md` supports registration-review worthiness for selected VoV candidates, while preserving unresolved contamination-reference limitations.
- `docs/research_notes/ohlcv_volatility_of_volatility_bounded_refinement_research_review_v1.md` requires redundancy checks against volatility compression, hostile/stress repair, persistence/rank stability, plain reversal, and volume-shock reversal.

Scientific stance: relative VoV is deferred. It may become valuable as an interpretive layer, but it must not presume independence from existing VoV, volatility compression, or stress-repair evidence.

## 9. Relative Liquidity And Participation Hypotheses

Peer-relative liquidity and participation mechanisms ask whether trading participation repairs or deteriorates differently from valid peers.

Hypotheses include:

- participation recovery versus peers;
- volume repair versus peers;
- relative liquidity withdrawal;
- relative trading-intensity normalization;
- peer-wide liquidity stress versus firm-specific liquidity stress;
- relative breadth participation where scientifically meaningful.

Overlap with existing evidence is material. Participation and liquidity repair already form part of the strongest current Project Underdog evidence base. A peer-relative liquidity mechanism must therefore show that peer context changes interpretation rather than merely re-computing volume or participation ranks.

Permitted future use: secondary module or contamination reference after the first repair/stabilization module is conceptually tested against Workstream 5 orthogonality requirements.

## 10. Relative Volatility And Compression Hypotheses

Relative volatility hypotheses ask whether a security normalizes, compresses, expands, or remains unstable differently from valid peers.

Hypotheses include:

- volatility compression faster than peers;
- failure to compress while peers stabilize;
- relative volatility expansion;
- peer-wide versus idiosyncratic volatility normalization;
- persistence of relative volatility state.

The likely relationship to existing volatility-compression evidence is close. A future hypothesis must distinguish relative volatility behavior from own-security volatility compression, stress state, VoV, and broad market volatility regime. Without that distinction, relative volatility is diagnostic-only.

## 11. Relative Transition And Regime Hypotheses

Relative transition hypotheses focus on ordering:

- entering repair before peers;
- exiting stress before peers;
- remaining unresolved while peers normalize;
- peer-relative transition-state divergence;
- transition synchronization;
- transition-order effects.

Scientific promise: transition order may help separate early idiosyncratic repair from common normalization.

Primary risks:

- overlap with prior transition-state work;
- overlap with leadership-rotation narratives;
- hindsight selection of transition dates;
- excessive horizon search.

Transition hypotheses are plausible only under frozen-horizon discipline and predeclared context windows.

## 12. Relative Leadership Hypotheses

Peer-relative leadership must be heavily constrained by the negative Non-Hostile Leadership Rotation findings.

Distinctions:

- broad leadership rotation: remains rejected or parked as an active path;
- post-stress peer leadership: plausible only as a narrow repair-order question;
- repair leadership: plausible if anchored to common stress and peer recovery;
- persistence leadership: plausible only if separated from rank persistence and momentum;
- transition leadership: plausible only with strict anti-NHLR framing;
- temporary relative outperformance: diagnostic unless tied to a defensible mechanism.

Preserved negative evidence:

- `docs/research_notes/ohlcv_non_hostile_transition_and_leadership_rotation_negative_result_review_v1.md` classifies the module as `FAMILY_PARKED_INVERSION_DIAGNOSTIC_OPTIONAL`; broad leadership rotation should not be continued or refined as a rescue path.

Scientific stance: leadership is not a primary module. Only post-stress repair leadership within valid peers may remain conceptually permissible, and even that must be treated as a constrained subcase of repair/stabilization asymmetry.

## 13. Relative Dispersion And Convergence Hypotheses

Peer dispersion and convergence ask whether the state of a valid peer group changes the meaning of individual security behavior.

Hypotheses include:

- widening peer dispersion;
- narrowing peer dispersion;
- convergence after stress;
- failure to converge;
- asymmetric outliers;
- peer-relative dispersion conditioned on repair or volatility state.

Preserved negative evidence:

- `docs/research_notes/dispersion_path_dependence_governance_review_v1.md` parks Dispersion Path-Dependence and preserves negative evidence.
- `docs/research_notes/event_clustering_governance_review_v1.md` parks Event Clustering and warns against event/topology mechanisms unsupported by evidence.

Scientific stance: dispersion/convergence is deferred and must not rename prior rejected mechanisms. It may later serve as a context state for repair, not as an unconstrained new discovery family.

## 14. Common-Shock Versus Idiosyncratic-Shock Science

Peer-relative interpretation depends on shock attribution.

Shock classes:

- common market shock;
- common sector shock;
- common peer shock;
- idiosyncratic security shock;
- event-specific shock;
- mixed shock.

Scientific assumption required: the project must be able to identify whether the peer group was exposed to a comparable condition at the relevant time. Without that, "relative repair" could mean common beta recovery, own-security rebound, event reversal, or missing-context noise.

Peer-relative context may be useful because it can separate systematic recovery from security-specific recovery. That claim is valid only if peer membership, shock timing, security identity, market state, and event context are temporally valid and reproducible.

## 15. Peer-Relative Asymmetry Science

Asymmetry should be central rather than a later refinement. Positive and negative relative states need not have symmetric meaning.

Asymmetries:

- outperforming versus underperforming peers;
- recovering versus failing to recover;
- positive versus negative divergence;
- leadership emergence versus deterioration;
- peer recovery participation versus non-participation;
- temporary versus persistent divergence.

Potential scientific value:

- relative failure-to-recover may be more informative than relative strength;
- rapid repair after common stress may be more meaningful than ordinary outperformance;
- persistent weakness during peer recovery may identify specific fragility.

No thresholds are defined here. The key requirement is that asymmetry be specified before empirical review, not inferred after outcomes are observed.

## 16. Context-Conditional Hypothesis Science

Peer-relative mechanisms may depend on:

- hostile versus non-hostile market state;
- stress versus normal state;
- volatility regime;
- liquidity regime;
- participation regime;
- transition state;
- sector-wide stress;
- peer-wide stress;
- event context.

Conditioning is scientifically necessary when a mechanism means different things in different states. For example, relative repair after common stress differs from relative outperformance during calm conditions.

Conditioning becomes dangerous when it fragments hypotheses into post hoc explanations. Platform v2 discipline requires predeclared contexts, frozen horizons, and clear learning objectives.

## 17. Horizon Science

Peer-relative hypotheses require horizon logic before formulas exist.

Conceptual horizon classes:

- immediate response;
- short-horizon repair;
- medium-horizon stabilization;
- persistent relative state;
- horizon mismatch;
- mechanism-specific horizon logic.

Risks:

- searching across too many horizons;
- choosing horizons after seeing results;
- mixing observation and forecast horizons;
- using peer context with a different known-date than the signal date.

Frozen-horizon discipline is preserved. This note chooses no numerical horizons.

## 18. Hypothesis Independence Framework

Two peer-relative hypotheses are distinct only if their difference is scientific, not merely computational.

Assessment dimensions:

- mechanism;
- context;
- direction;
- state transition;
- temporal behavior;
- economic interpretation;
- existing-family overlap;
- expected empirical signature.

Possible outcomes:

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `SCIENTIFICALLY_DISTINCT` | The hypothesis has a separate mechanism and expected signature. |
| `CONDITIONALLY_DISTINCT` | It may be separate under specified contexts or controls. |
| `LIKELY_REDUNDANT` | It probably duplicates another peer-relative or existing OHLCV family. |
| `REFINEMENT_ONLY` | It narrows an existing mechanism but does not define a new family. |
| `REINTERPRETATION_ONLY` | It changes interpretation of an existing signal without creating a new hypothesis. |
| `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` | The repository does not support a distinct scientific definition. |

These are conceptual scientific outcomes, not empirical conclusions.

## 19. Hypothesis Prioritization Framework

Future peer-relative hypotheses should be prioritized by:

- scientific novelty;
- plausibility;
- relationship to validated or strongly supported evidence;
- expected incremental information;
- redundancy risk;
- contamination risk;
- context dependency;
- identity dependency;
- data burden;
- falsifiability;
- interpretability;
- negative-evidence value.

Priority should favor mechanisms that are close enough to existing evidence to be plausible but far enough from it to test a genuine new axis. Breadth is less valuable than disciplined information gain.

## 21. Hypothesis Ambiguity Taxonomy

| Ambiguity class | Scientific meaning | Required clarification | Permitted use | Prohibited use | Downstream consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mechanism ambiguity | The causal or behavioral story is unclear. | Define the mechanism and competing explanations. | Conceptual discussion. | Formula or module design. | Blocks acceptance. |
| Peer-definition ambiguity | The relevant peers are not scientifically defined. | Resolve economic context and peer eligibility. | Diagnostic examples. | Peer-relative claims. | Blocks empirical work. |
| Context ambiguity | Market, stress, sector, or peer state is unclear. | Define conditioning context. | Hypothesis refinement. | Post hoc context selection. | Requires Workstream 5 review. |
| Temporal ambiguity | Timing of peer membership, stress, or observation is unclear. | Identify known dates and observation dates. | Conceptual scenario design. | PIT claims. | Fail-closed for empirical use. |
| Directionality ambiguity | It is unclear whether high or low relative state is expected to matter. | Predeclare sign logic conceptually. | Diagnostic review. | Sign flipping after results. | Blocks design acceptance. |
| Redundancy ambiguity | Overlap with existing families is unresolved. | Compare against existing-family mechanisms. | Workstream 5 input. | Claiming novelty. | Blocks formula/panel design. |
| Causality ambiguity | The interpretation implies unsupported cause. | Reframe as association or mechanism hypothesis. | Scientific hypothesis. | Causal claim. | Requires careful language. |
| Interpretation ambiguity | Same observation supports multiple meanings. | Define alternative explanations. | Conditional hypothesis. | Strong conclusion. | May become diagnostic-only. |
| Contamination ambiguity | Look-ahead, source, identity, or family leakage may exist. | Contamination assessment. | Workstream 5 review. | Empirical use. | Blocks module advancement. |
| Fatal hypothesis ambiguity | The hypothesis cannot be made falsifiable or peer-valid. | Foundational redefinition. | None beyond rejection record. | Any advancement. | Reject. |

## 22. Peer-Relative Hypothesis Acceptance Framework

Each future hypothesis should be assessed for:

- scientific mechanism;
- economic interpretation;
- peer-context dependency;
- identity dependency;
- temporal coherence;
- existing-family overlap;
- falsifiability;
- contamination exposure;
- negative-evidence conflict;
- expected knowledge value.

Possible outcomes:

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `HYPOTHESIS_ACCEPTED_FOR_FUTURE_DESIGN` | Conceptually ready for later formula/panel design once all prerequisites exist. |
| `HYPOTHESIS_CONDITIONALLY_ACCEPTED` | Scientifically plausible but dependent on unresolved authority, identity, context, or contamination evidence. |
| `HYPOTHESIS_REFINEMENT_REQUIRED` | Mechanism is plausible but too broad or ambiguous. |
| `HYPOTHESIS_DIAGNOSTIC_ONLY` | Useful for interpretation or stress-testing, not as an authoritative research input. |
| `HYPOTHESIS_REJECTED` | Conflicts with evidence, lacks mechanism, or duplicates rejected work. |
| `INSUFFICIENT_SCIENTIFIC_DEFINITION` | Not enough conceptual specificity to evaluate. |

No formulas, candidate IDs, registries, panels, or empirical records are created by this framework.

## 23. Fail-Closed Conditions

A peer-relative hypothesis must not advance if any of the following apply:

- no defensible economic mechanism;
- no valid peer interpretation;
- dependence on current-state metadata;
- retrospective peer assignment;
- unresolved identity ambiguity;
- no plausible incremental information beyond existing families;
- direct contradiction with preserved negative evidence;
- unfalsifiable interpretation;
- excessive context fragmentation;
- horizon defined only after observing results;
- dependence on unsupported causal claims;
- silent reliance on unavailable external evidence;
- disguised duplication of an existing family;
- peer membership not PIT-valid;
- security identity and economic-company identity are conflated;
- source authority is not role-specific;
- missing delisted entities or terminal states would bias interpretation;
- contamination cannot be assessed.
