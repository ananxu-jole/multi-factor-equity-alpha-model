# Project Underdog - OHLCV Non-Hostile Transition and Leadership Rotation Discovery Design v1

## SECTION 1 - Executive Summary

This note designs the next major OHLCV-only alpha discovery program for Project Underdog. The proposed frontier is non-hostile market transition and leadership rotation: the study of orderly capital migration, healthy trend handoffs, gradual participation expansion, and emerging leadership behavior outside hostile, panic, drawdown, or stress-repair states.

This is a research design task only. No code was implemented, no candidate panels were generated, no discovery was run, no refinement was run, no validation was run, no governance was modified, nothing was registered to production, and no ML was implemented.

Research objective:

- Identify whether healthy market transitions and leadership rotation can form a distinct OHLCV-only alpha family.
- Expand Project Underdog beyond its current concentration in hostile/stress repair, post-drawdown persistence, and h20 stabilization behavior.
- Design a disciplined future discovery program that tests the economic mechanism without relying on CRSP/PIT metadata, sector labels, peer groups, or economic context.

Economic motivation:

Markets do not only create alpha during repair from stress. Capital can migrate gradually from older leaders to newer leaders while broad market conditions remain neutral, improving, or orderly. If this migration is observable through OHLCV behavior, then securities showing early leadership emergence, smooth rank improvement, volume-supported trend handoff, or broad participation expansion may contain forward-looking information distinct from stress absorption.

Expected contribution:

- A new research family that is independent of paused CRSP/PIT work.
- A non-hostile complement to the established stress-repair family.
- A possible bridge between rank-coherence, participation, dispersion, and transition-state research without repeating any one of those tracks.
- A feasible OHLCV-only path toward alpha-family diversification before ML or metadata-aware research becomes appropriate.

Relationship to existing families:

- Hostile/stress repair remains the established core family and the main contamination benchmark.
- Persistence and rank-coherence remain conditional candidate-lineages, but they are not enough to establish broad family diversity.
- Prior transition-state alpha discovery failed when it focused on stress absorption as a standalone alpha.
- This frontier should explicitly avoid stress-repair gates and instead test whether orderly, non-hostile leadership transitions have their own alpha content.

## SECTION 2 - Economic Hypothesis

The core hypothesis is that alpha may arise when market leadership changes hands in an orderly way before that change is fully reflected in broad price momentum or conventional trend measures.

Orderly leadership rotation:

- Leadership rotation occurs when securities with improving relative behavior begin replacing older leaders without a broad market breakdown.
- In healthy rotation, new leadership may emerge through persistent but non-explosive rank improvement, improving participation, and controlled volatility rather than sharp reversal or panic recovery.
- This may create alpha because capital migration is gradual, institutional positioning changes slowly, and broad market aggregates may hide the cross-sectional handoff.

Gradual capital migration:

- Capital often moves from crowded or mature winners into securities with improving liquidity, better participation, and healthier trend structure.
- OHLCV data can proxy this migration through volume quality, participation expansion, trend consistency, and changing cross-sectional ranks.
- A security does not need to be recovering from distress to benefit; it may simply be moving from overlooked to newly favored.

Healthy trend transitions:

- A healthy transition is not a rebound from panic. It is a move from neutral, consolidating, or orderly trend behavior into stronger leadership.
- The candidate mechanism should favor transitions with controlled range behavior, broadening participation, and non-disruptive volatility adaptation.
- The expected signal is not "oversold recovery"; it is "early orderly improvement."

Sector or group leadership changes observable through OHLCV:

- The project cannot use PIT sector, industry, or peer metadata in this program.
- However, broad-market leadership changes can still be approximated through cross-sectional OHLCV behavior: rank movement, breadth contribution, participation changes, and distribution of trend strength across the universe.
- The future design should treat these as universe-relative leadership proxies, not true sector-relative or peer-relative signals.

Broad participation without market stress:

- Healthy leadership rotation should show participation expansion without requiring hostile trend, weak breadth, panic, drawdown, or repair conditions.
- The strongest future concepts should separate "more investors are accumulating this name" from "this name is bouncing after stress."

Why this may create independent alpha:

- It targets capital allocation shifts rather than stress recovery.
- It may activate in neutral or improving regimes where current stress-repair candidates are less relevant.
- It can create horizon and state diversification if evidence appears at h5/h10 as well as h20.
- It tests whether rank and participation changes forecast future leadership before they become obvious momentum signals.

## SECTION 3 - Distinction from Existing Families

Hostile/stress repair:

- Existing strength is concentrated in securities repairing participation, breadth, liquidity, or volatility after hostile or fragile conditions.
- The new frontier must avoid hostile/stress gates as primary activation logic.
- A successful result should remain useful when stress-repair conditions are absent.

Persistence:

- Persistence studies rank stability after drawdown, especially whether low rank churn predicts future returns.
- The new frontier is not post-drawdown persistence. It should study leadership emergence and handoff in non-hostile conditions.
- Persistence may serve as a redundancy reference, not a design template.

Rank-coherence:

- Rank-coherence studies coherent rank-turnover resilience and stable cross-sectional rank structure.
- Leadership rotation may use rank behavior conceptually, but it should not simply reward existing rank stability.
- The new family should care about transition quality and leadership transfer, not just low churn or coherent rank persistence.

Participation/liquidity:

- Participation and liquidity research has been most successful inside repair regimes.
- The new frontier may use participation concepts, but only as non-hostile accumulation or broadening behavior.
- A future candidate that works only because it reproduces participation repair should be treated as redundant.

Volatility adaptation:

- Existing volatility compression and shock-absorption work is largely stress-adjacent.
- This frontier should examine calm-to-expansion, orderly volatility adaptation, and range behavior without requiring prior volatility panic.
- Volatility should be a quality-control dimension for transition health, not the whole alpha thesis.

Transition-state work:

- Prior standalone transition-state discovery rejected ten stress-absorption candidates.
- The lesson is that generic transition-state behavior is too broad and often collapses into stress repair.
- The new direction narrows the transition-state idea to non-hostile leadership rotation, which is economically more specific and less repair-dependent.

Why this is genuinely new:

- It asks a different question: not "who repairs best after damage?" but "who becomes a new leader during orderly capital migration?"
- It targets neutral and improving regimes rather than hostile or post-drawdown states.
- It makes leadership handoff the mechanism rather than a side effect of recovery.
- It can be tested with current OHLCV infrastructure while preserving a clean boundary from metadata-dependent peer-relative research.

## SECTION 4 - Candidate Concept Space

The future discovery program should define broad concept categories only at this stage. No formulas, implementation details, parameter grids, or candidate panels are specified in this note.

Potential concept categories:

- Gradual leadership emergence: securities that move from neutral or middling cross-sectional standing toward leadership in an orderly, sustained way.
- Smooth relative-strength transfer: securities that improve relative behavior while older leaders flatten, weaken, or lose participation, using only universe-relative OHLCV proxies.
- Orderly participation expansion: rising volume or participation quality without price exhaustion, panic rebound, or stress-repair gating.
- Leadership persistence after healthy transitions: securities that become leaders after a calm or neutral transition and then maintain leadership without excessive churn.
- Rotation acceleration/deceleration: changes in the pace of leadership migration that may identify early or late stages of capital rotation.
- Volume-supported leadership changes: emerging strength accompanied by non-disruptive volume support rather than one-day shocks.
- Trend handoff dynamics: transitions from consolidation to trend participation, or from broad neutral behavior to emerging trend leadership.
- Calm-to-expansion transitions: early participation or trend improvement before broad volatility or range expansion becomes disorderly.
- Non-hostile breadth contribution: securities that begin contributing positively to universe breadth without relying on weak-breadth repair states.
- Rotation quality diagnostics: measures intended only to distinguish orderly rotation from unstable churn, not to become standalone alpha concepts.

Design boundary:

- These categories are a concept map, not a candidate list.
- A later discovery specification may choose a small subset.
- The next task should translate this concept space into a predeclared candidate panel only after separate anti-redundancy and anti-mining controls are specified.

## SECTION 5 - Research Constraints

The program must remain OHLCV-only.

Allowed research substrate:

- Open, high, low, close, volume, adjusted price behavior, returns, realized volatility, range behavior, volume behavior, breadth-like universe aggregates, and cross-sectional ranks derived from those fields.
- Existing research infrastructure for candidate scoring, horizon review, redundancy diagnostics, active coverage, and state attribution.

Disallowed dependencies:

- No PIT metadata.
- No CRSP/PIT source work.
- No sector metadata.
- No industry metadata.
- No peer-relative construction.
- No economic-context dependency.
- No static snapshot sector or industry labels as a substitute for PIT metadata.
- No source loading or metadata construction.
- No ML.

Conceptual exclusions:

- Do not use hostile, panic, weak-breadth, drawdown, or stress-repair conditions as the primary thesis.
- Do not create formulas in this design note.
- Do not define candidate panels in this design note.
- Do not tune against known validation outcomes from persistence, rank-coherence, dispersion, or stress-repair work.
- Do not treat broad market stress absorption as leadership rotation.

The future discovery design should include explicit contamination checks against:

- Hostile/stress repair anchors.
- Participation/breadth repair anchors.
- Persistence conditional candidates.
- Rank-coherence conditional candidates.
- Dispersion diagnostic references.
- Volatility compression and shock-absorption references.

## SECTION 6 - Expected Risks

Redundancy risk:

- The largest risk is that leadership rotation becomes another label for rank-coherence, participation repair, or stress-repair stabilization.
- Any future candidate that activates mainly during hostile, recovery, or drawdown states should be considered contaminated unless the evidence clearly supports a separate mechanism.

Weak signal risk:

- Leadership rotation may be economically real but hard to isolate using only broad OHLCV data.
- Without sector or peer context, true rotation may be diluted by universe-wide trend and momentum effects.

Trend-following overlap:

- A naive implementation may become ordinary momentum or trend continuation.
- Future designs must distinguish healthy transition quality from simple recent strength.

Momentum overlap:

- Smooth leadership emergence can look like momentum by construction.
- Future redundancy review should include momentum-like behavior and rank persistence controls so the program does not overstate novelty.

Implementation complexity:

- Even without PIT metadata, conceptually clean rotation measures can become complex if they require many cross-sectional ranks, window comparisons, and state exclusions.
- The future candidate panel should stay small and interpretable.

Validation challenges:

- The signal may be state-dependent with sparse activation.
- Evidence may concentrate in a few windows or horizons.
- Positive results may be vulnerable to regime-specific leadership cycles.
- Strong h20 behavior alone would not be enough if h5/h10 evidence is absent and window concentration is high.

Interpretation risk:

- Without PIT sector/industry labels, the program cannot claim true sector rotation.
- It can only claim OHLCV-observable leadership rotation at the broad universe level.

## SECTION 7 - Success Criteria

A successful new alpha family would need to satisfy conceptual, empirical, and process criteria.

Conceptual independence:

- The mechanism must be orderly leadership rotation or healthy non-hostile transition, not repair after stress.
- It must be explainable as capital migration, leadership emergence, or trend handoff.
- It must not depend on CRSP/PIT metadata, static sector labels, or peer-relative construction.

Economic plausibility:

- The behavior should map to a credible market process: gradual accumulation, broadening participation, early leadership emergence, or controlled transition from neutral to improving conditions.
- The evidence should be consistent with non-hostile regimes rather than dominated by panic recovery.

Diversification potential:

- Future candidates should show low redundancy versus hostile/stress repair.
- They should also be meaningfully distinct from persistence and rank-coherence conditional candidate-lineages.
- The family should ideally activate in different state contexts from the current established inventory.

Feasibility using current infrastructure:

- The program must be executable using existing OHLCV panels and research harnesses.
- It should use established discovery, redundancy, coverage, and horizon-review processes.
- It should not require new data sources, metadata lineage, or ML infrastructure.

Empirical success criteria for a later discovery pass:

- Positive h5/h10 or h10/h20 IC evidence, not a single isolated horizon.
- Reasonable positive IC rate at primary horizons.
- Adequate active coverage and no extremely narrow activation slice unless explicitly justified.
- No single-window dominance.
- Low contamination versus stress-repair, persistence, rank-coherence, and dispersion references.
- A small predeclared candidate set with no broad parameter search.

Process success criteria:

- Candidate concepts are predeclared before discovery.
- Candidate count is small enough to limit mining risk.
- Redundancy controls are defined before results are known.
- Any future refinement must be based on predeclared diagnostics, not after-the-fact horizon chasing.

## SECTION 8 - Future Discovery Scope

Recommended discovery scope:

- Approximate exploratory concepts: 8 to 12.
- Candidate families represented: 4 to 6 broad concept categories.
- Primary horizons: h5, h10, and h20, with h10/h20 as the likely strategic read and h5 used to detect earlier rotation behavior.
- Primary comparison references: stress-repair anchors, persistence conditional candidate, rank-coherence conditional candidate, dispersion diagnostics, and simple momentum/trend proxies if available in the research framework.

Breadth versus depth:

- Favor breadth across distinct concepts over many parameter variants of one idea.
- Avoid more than two close siblings per concept category.
- Include diagnostic controls only if they help distinguish leadership rotation from trend/momentum or repair.

Anti-overfitting controls:

- Predeclare candidate count and concept categories.
- Avoid formula tuning after the first discovery read.
- Avoid state exclusions derived from known validation weaknesses in existing families.
- Avoid optimizing for h20 alone.
- Require active coverage and window concentration diagnostics.
- Require redundancy review before any refinement eligibility recommendation.

Redundancy screening strategy:

- Compare candidate outputs against hostile/stress-repair references.
- Compare against persistence and rank-coherence conditional candidates.
- Compare against dispersion and volatility normalization diagnostics.
- Review state attribution to confirm non-hostile activation.
- Flag candidates with high sibling correlation, high stress-state concentration, or strong momentum-only behavior.

Recommended future outcome classifications:

- `discovery_candidate`: credible evidence and distinct mechanism.
- `diagnostic_only`: useful state or interpretation tool but not an alpha candidate.
- `redundant_with_existing_family`: evidence mostly replicates stress repair, persistence, rank-coherence, or ordinary momentum.
- `reject_research`: weak, unstable, overly narrow, or economically unclear.

This note does not select or design actual candidates.

## SECTION 9 - Relationship to Future PIT Work

This family should remain fully executable today without PIT metadata.

Current OHLCV-only role:

- Test broad universe-level leadership transitions.
- Study rank, trend, volume, participation, range, and volatility behavior without sector or peer labels.
- Establish whether non-hostile leadership rotation has standalone alpha potential before adding metadata complexity.

Future PIT-enhanced role:

- If CRSP/PIT metadata later resumes, this family could be enriched with sector, industry, size, or peer-relative context.
- PIT metadata could distinguish true sector rotation from broad universe leadership changes.
- Ticker and security lineage could improve historical continuity for long-lived leadership studies.
- Peer groups could help separate within-sector leadership emergence from market-wide momentum.

Boundary until PIT evidence exists:

- Do not call this program sector rotation.
- Do not infer industry or peer leadership from static metadata.
- Do not use static sector labels as a shortcut.
- Do not reopen CRSP planning as part of this research program.

The correct framing is OHLCV-observable leadership rotation now, with possible PIT-aware interpretation later.

## SECTION 10 - Final Recommendation

1. Is this a sufficiently distinct alpha family?

Yes, as a research design frontier. Non-hostile transition and leadership rotation targets a different economic mechanism from hostile/stress repair, persistence, and rank-coherence. It is distinct enough to justify a dedicated discovery design if the next step preserves strict anti-redundancy controls.

2. Why is it worth pursuing now?

It directly addresses Project Underdog's main bottleneck: alpha-family concentration. It is independent of the paused CRSP/PIT track, feasible with current OHLCV infrastructure, and economically plausible as a capital-migration and leadership-handoff mechanism.

3. What are the largest research risks?

The main risks are redundancy with rank-coherence or momentum, accidental drift into stress repair, weak signal quality without sector/peer context, and overfitting through overly complex transition definitions.

4. Should it become the next major discovery program?

Yes. It should become the next major non-CRSP discovery program, but only after a separate discovery specification freezes candidate count, concept categories, allowed inputs, redundancy references, and outcome classifications.

5. What should the next Codex task be?

The next Codex task should be **Project Underdog - OHLCV Non-Hostile Transition and Leadership Rotation Discovery Specification v1**. It should translate this design into a small, predeclared discovery plan with concept categories, candidate-count limits, allowed OHLCV inputs, redundancy-screening requirements, artifact expectations, and fail-closed restrictions. It should not implement code, generate candidates, run discovery, run refinement, run validation, modify governance, register production artifacts, or implement ML.
