# Project Underdog - Alpha Family Inventory and Diversification Review v1

Date: 2026-06-20

Scope: review-only strategic assessment after the persistence and rank-coherence validation cycles. No discovery, refinement, validation, governance mutation, threshold change, production registration, ML implementation, or candidate promotion/demotion was performed.

## SECTION 1 - Executive Summary

Project Underdog has made real progress from a single-family research program toward a small portfolio of alpha-family threads, but the portfolio remains concentrated. The strongest evidence still belongs to hostile/stress-repair behavior, especially participation and breadth repair under adverse regimes. Persistence and rank-coherence have both survived validation as `CONDITIONAL VALIDATION CANDIDATE` lineages, which improves diversification, but neither has matured into a broad validated family.

Current maturity assessment:

- Established research umbrella: hostile/stress repair, including participation repair and related stabilization behavior.
- Conditional candidate-lineages: persistence and rank-coherence.
- Exploratory but not validated family: dispersion.
- Diagnostic or parked research: structural interaction, recovery-quality targets, economic-context enrichment, non-price liquidity repair, transition-state variants, peer-group diagnostics, OHLCV non-hostile transition/leadership rotation, and several weak volatility/participation interaction branches.
- Post-review update: the OHLCV Non-Hostile Transition and Leadership Rotation cycle is now classified `FAMILY_PARKED_INVERSION_DIAGNOSTIC_OPTIONAL`; all nine approved candidates were rejected, no refinement is recommended, and panels/IC artifacts remain archived as research evidence.

Diversification assessment:

The inventory now contains three meaningful alpha-family axes if counted conservatively: hostile/stress repair, persistence, and rank-coherence. Only the first is established. The other two are conditional and candidate-level. Dispersion remains the most important explored-but-unproven quantitative family, while peer-relative and economic-context-dependent behavior remains the largest structurally independent gap.

Major strengths:

- The hostile/stress-repair family has multiple candidates, explicit monitoring, and one cleanest current anchor in `participation_breadth_repair_under_hostile_trend`.
- Persistence survived validation with low stress-repair contamination and useful h10/h20 behavior.
- Rank-coherence survived validation conditionally and reduced direct persistence overlap in the strongest refined candidate.
- The research process has become more disciplined: discovery, refinement, validation, and review artifacts are now auditable and separated.

Major weaknesses:

- The active inventory remains dominated by hostile/stress, h20, repair, and stabilization behavior.
- Persistence and rank-coherence are still single-lineage conditional results, not broad family proof.
- Rank-coherence retains stress-repair similarity and sibling duplicate risk.
- Dispersion is distinct but empirically weak, especially at h20.
- Peer-relative and economic-context alpha is blocked from validation-quality use until point-in-time metadata exists.

## SECTION 3 - Family Dependency Review

Family overlap is the central portfolio risk. The active research inventory has low pairwise signal correlation in some artifacts, but low correlation does not by itself prove family independence when candidates share state activation, h20 horizon focus, or repair mechanics.

Stress-repair overlap:

- Participation repair, breadth repair, liquidity repair, volatility compression, shock absorption, and many transition-state candidates are best viewed as expressions of the same broad hostile/stress-repair umbrella.
- The current inventory's strongest evidence is concentrated in hostile, weak-breadth, panic/liquidity, drawdown, or stabilization states.
- Volatility compression is useful, but it is not yet independent enough from stress repair to count as a separate established family.

Persistence overlap:

- Persistence is distinct from the hostile/stress-repair family at the artifact level. The validation candidate had maximum stress-repair reference correlation of `0.117322`.
- Persistence remains adjacent to drawdown and post-repair behavior, so validation should not be overread as broad non-state alpha.
- It is best counted as one conditional candidate-lineage rather than a complete family.

Rank-coherence overlap:

- Rank-coherence is distinct from persistence in the final validation contamination review, with the primary candidate showing maximum persistence correlation of `0.130705`.
- It is less clean against stress-repair references: the primary candidate had maximum stress-repair correlation of `0.393906`, a formal validation risk.
- High sibling rank-coherence correlation of `0.923052` means the validated evidence is lineage continuity, not family breadth.

Dispersion overlap:

- Dispersion appears conceptually independent and had modest contamination against existing families, but its alpha evidence remains weak.
- The current dispersion thread is not a disguised persistence or stress-repair family, but it has not yet proven robust enough to carry portfolio diversification.

Which families are truly independent?

- Hostile/stress repair is independent as the primary established umbrella.
- Persistence is conditionally independent enough to count as a meaningful candidate-lineage.
- Rank-coherence is conditionally independent enough to remain active, but stress-repair similarity must be tested carefully in any future integration review.
- Dispersion is structurally independent but not yet empirically mature.

Which families may be different expressions of the same phenomenon?

- Participation repair, breadth repair, liquidity repair, volatility compression, shock absorption, and many transition-state candidates may be different expressions of stress repair and stabilization.
- Recovery-quality target lifts are diagnostic expressions of repair quality, not independent alpha families.
- Structural interaction candidates mostly recombine existing repair ingredients and should not be counted as new families.

## SECTION 4 - Diversification Gap Analysis

The largest remaining gaps are not just empty candidate slots; they are missing economic mechanisms that could reduce dependence on hostile/stress repair and h20 stabilization behavior.

Ranked gaps:

1. Peer-relative / economic-context-dependent behavior.
   - Strategic value: highest.
   - Independence: high if point-in-time metadata is available.
   - Current status: blocked from validation-quality alpha work because current metadata is static-snapshot diagnostic-only.
   - Why it matters: it could test whether a stock is improving or degrading relative to true economic peers, rather than relative to the whole universe or stress state.

2. Dispersion and cross-sectional instability.
   - Strategic value: high.
   - Independence: high.
   - Current status: explored but weak.
   - Why it matters: dispersion behavior is one of the cleanest ways to move away from repair, persistence, and rank-turnover lineages.

3. Transition-state dynamics outside hostile/stress repair.
   - Strategic value: medium-high.
   - Independence: medium if designed away from repair states.
   - Current status: partially explored, often stress-adjacent; the OHLCV-only non-hostile transition and leadership-rotation implementation has now been parked after broad negative IC evidence.
   - Why it matters: true calm-to-turbulent, compression-to-expansion, or leadership-transition states could add a new regime family.

4. Leadership rotation and cross-sectional leadership changes.
   - Strategic value: medium-high.
   - Independence: medium.
   - Current status: tested through the OHLCV Non-Hostile Transition and Leadership Rotation cycle and parked as `FAMILY_PARKED_INVERSION_DIAGNOSTIC_OPTIONAL`; any future work should be redesign-level or direction-inversion diagnostic only.
   - Why it matters: leadership rotation may capture a different phenomenon from rank stability or churn avoidance.

5. Participation breadth outside repair.
   - Strategic value: medium.
   - Independence: medium-low.
   - Current status: existing evidence is repair-heavy.
   - Why it matters: broader participation behavior could be useful if it escapes hostile/stress gating.

6. Volatility structure beyond compression.
   - Strategic value: medium.
   - Independence: medium-low using current data.
   - Current status: compression and shock absorption are repair-adjacent.
   - Why it matters: volatility slope, realized-volatility regime shifts, or volatility-of-volatility may add structure, but current work has not proven this.

7. Alternative targets and recovery quality.
   - Strategic value: diagnostic, not alpha-family priority.
   - Independence: low as a standalone family.
   - Current status: useful sidecar.
   - Why it matters: it helps interpret candidates but should not drive validation decisions.

## SECTION 5 - Family Portfolio Assessment

Viewed as a portfolio, Project Underdog is stronger than it was before the persistence and rank-coherence cycles, but still concentrated.

Concentration risk:

- High. The strongest family remains hostile/stress repair.
- The established inventory is mostly h20, repair, stabilization, weak-breadth, drawdown, or stress-state behavior.
- Persistence and rank-coherence add breadth but are still conditional and lineage-level.

Maturity distribution:

- Established: one broad umbrella, hostile/stress repair.
- Conditional: persistence and rank-coherence; volatility compression as a stress-adjacent subfamily.
- Exploratory: dispersion, transition-state beyond stress, volatility shock absorption.
- Diagnostic: economic context, peer-group substrate, recovery-quality targets, structural interaction.
- Rejected or parked: most structural interaction candidates and weak refined dispersion variants.

Robustness:

- Hostile/stress repair has the broadest and strongest robustness record.
- Persistence has better validation stability than rank-coherence, especially at h10.
- Rank-coherence survived validation conditionally, but h10 WFV weakness and concentration risks remain.
- Dispersion remains fragile.

Diversification quality:

- Improved, but not enough. The project has moved from one-family dependence to one established family plus two conditional satellites.
- The satellites matter, but they do not yet offset the concentration of the established research inventory.

If Project Underdog stopped today, the family inventory would be credible as a research program but not broadly diversified. It would have one durable core family and two promising conditional family threads. That is materially better than a single-family program, but still too narrow for ML, construction-layer integration, or claims of broad alpha-family coverage.

## SECTION 6 - ML Readiness Review

ML introduction is still premature.

Reasons:

- Family diversity is not yet sufficient. One established family and two conditional lineages create too little independent training structure.
- The most mature candidates are concentrated in repair/stabilization regimes, making ML likely to learn state exposure rather than robust alpha mechanisms.
- Persistence and rank-coherence are candidate-lineage evidence, not broad enough labels for a family-aware model.
- Peer-relative and economic-context features are not validation-ready because point-in-time metadata is not yet available.
- Alternative targets are diagnostic sidecars and should not become ML optimization targets.

Minimum family inventory before ML becomes productive:

- At least two established families with validation-quality evidence.
- At least two additional conditional families with distinct mechanisms, horizons, and state profiles.
- Documented contamination reviews showing low dependence on hostile/stress repair.
- Point-in-time metadata if peer-relative or economic-context features are included.
- Stable monitoring showing that current `WATCH_MONITOR` candidates are either accepted with guardrails or replaced by cleaner evidence.

ML should remain future-phase work. The next gains should come from better family breadth, not model complexity.

## SECTION 7 - Future Domain Expansion Review

Options-derived signals:

- Not ready. The current research program has not yet stabilized enough cross-sectional equity families to justify adding options data complexity.
- Prerequisites: options data quality review, point-in-time option-chain availability, survivorship-safe universe mapping, liquidity filters, and a clear hypothesis not reducible to realized volatility compression.

Fixed-income signals:

- Not ready. Fixed-income integration would introduce different data calendars, curve construction, issuer mapping, and macro exposure controls.
- Prerequisites: reliable issuer/security mapping, point-in-time curve data, sector and credit-quality metadata, and a governance design separating equity alpha from cross-asset context.

Macro signals:

- Future-phase. Macro context could help state attribution, but it should not be introduced as a broad overlay before existing family concentration is reduced.
- Prerequisites: point-in-time macro releases, release-lag handling, revision policy, state-definition governance, and evidence that macro context adds information beyond existing stress detectors.

Alternative data:

- Future-phase. Alternative data would add high leakage and survivorship risk unless the core validation process is already mature.
- Prerequisites: timestamp lineage, vendor coverage diagnostics, universe overlap review, availability-lag modeling, and strict validation isolation.

Future domain expansion should wait until Project Underdog has a more balanced equity-alpha family inventory and a stronger point-in-time metadata substrate.

## SECTION 8 - Recommended Research Frontier

Recommended highest-priority next frontier: peer-relative / economic-context-dependent behavior.

Rationale:

This is the largest strategically independent gap. It could test whether rank, persistence, dispersion, participation, or recovery behavior matters relative to true economic peers rather than broad cross-sectional ranks. That is a different source of information from hostile/stress repair, persistence, and rank-coherence, and it could eventually support several independent families: peer-relative resilience, sector-relative exhaustion, industry-relative stabilization, and leadership rotation inside economic cohorts.

Expected diversification benefit:

- High. A successful peer-relative family would directly reduce dependence on market-wide stress states and universe-wide ranks.
- It could also clarify whether current rank-coherence evidence is general cross-sectional structure or a hidden peer-relative effect.

Expected independence from existing families:

- High if built on point-in-time peer groups and residual behavior.
- Medium if implemented using static metadata or broad sector labels, which would not be validation-quality and could create false diversification.

Expected implementation difficulty:

- Medium-high. The research infrastructure has a diagnostic substrate, but validation-quality alpha work is blocked until point-in-time sector, industry, size, and peer-group metadata exists.

Expected research value:

- Very high. This frontier addresses the largest missing class of alpha behavior and creates a path toward richer family definitions without ML or new asset classes.

Important constraint:

The next task should not execute peer-relative alpha discovery immediately. The correct next step is a readiness and design task that audits point-in-time metadata availability, defines validation-safe peer-relative transforms, and specifies the smallest possible discovery batch only after metadata readiness is established.

## SECTION 10 - Final Recommendation

1. How many meaningful alpha families currently exist?

Three meaningful alpha-family axes currently exist: one established hostile/stress-repair umbrella and two conditional candidate-lineages, persistence and rank-coherence. Dispersion is a fourth exploratory axis, but it is not yet strong enough to count as a meaningful validated or conditional family.

2. Is Project Underdog still too concentrated?

Yes. The project is less concentrated than before, but it remains too dependent on hostile/stress repair, h20 stabilization, and repair-adjacent state behavior.

3. What is the biggest remaining diversification gap?

The biggest strategic gap is peer-relative / economic-context-dependent alpha. It is the most likely path to a genuinely independent family, but it is blocked until point-in-time metadata is available.

4. Is ML still premature?

Yes. ML should wait until the project has broader family diversity, cleaner point-in-time context data, and more than one established family.

5. Are options/fixed income still future-phase work?

Yes. Options, fixed income, macro, and alternative data should remain future-phase work until the equity alpha-family inventory is more balanced and the metadata/governance prerequisites are stronger.

6. What should the next Codex task be?

The next Codex task should be **Project Underdog - Peer-Relative Economic Context Readiness and Alpha Frontier Design v1**. It should be design-only and should audit point-in-time metadata readiness, define validation-safe peer-relative feature families, specify anti-leakage controls, and propose the smallest disciplined discovery batch if the metadata prerequisite is satisfied. It should not run discovery, refinement, validation, governance mutation, threshold changes, production registration, ML, or candidate promotion/demotion.
