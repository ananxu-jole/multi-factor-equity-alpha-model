## SECTION 4 - Successful Mechanisms

Successful or useful mechanisms:

- Conditional repair under hostile or weak-breadth states.
- Breadth repair with low extension and low turnover.
- Participation/liquidity repair in adverse states.
- Volatility compression after stress when activation and rebalance controls reduce rank churn.
- Post-drawdown rank persistence with churn discipline.
- Rank-turnover resilience and overlap-adjusted rank-coherence at h20.
- Volume-shock reversal as a controlled reference, though not a clean orthogonal discovery frontier.

Shared traits of successful evidence:

- State dependence matters.
- h10/h20 horizons remain most informative, but h20 concentration is a recurring risk.
- Low churn and low extension controls often improve interpretability.
- Broad parameter expansion has been less valuable than small, mechanism-specific refinements.

## SECTION 5 - Failed Or Parked Mechanisms

Failed or parked mechanisms:

- Naive continuation, leadership, and rank-quality structures from earlier Track B work.
- Broad non-hostile leadership rotation as implemented in the OHLCV cycle.
- Simple dispersion rising-state narrowing.
- Structural interaction candidates that merely recombined repair ingredients.
- Event-quality continuation and gap-followthrough variants with weak state coverage.
- Non-price liquidity repair without a sharper state thesis.
- Static metadata and peer-relative alpha work, blocked by point-in-time source limitations.

Lessons:

- Non-hostile leadership evidence was not simply weak; it was directionally adverse at h10/h20.
- Dispersion is distinct but fragile; simple acceleration is not enough.
- More participation/liquidity repair variants will probably increase redundancy.
- Peer-relative research should not be forced through static metadata.
- Inversion diagnostics may be useful, but should not become stealth refinement of a rejected family.

## SECTION 8 - Redundancy Assessment

Primary redundancy risks:

- Stress-repair overlap: any volatility or event-clustering family can accidentally become another panic/drawdown repair candidate.
- Rank-coherence overlap: dispersion stability and rank-turnover resilience can look similar if both reward coherent cross-sectional structure.
- Persistence overlap: path-dependent stabilization after drawdown can become persistence with extra filters.
- Volume-shock reversal overlap: event clustering around volume shocks may become a disguised reversal/liquidity-flow variant.
- Parked OHLCV leadership overlap: non-hostile transition language can drift back toward the rejected leadership-rotation family.

Controls required in the next discovery design:

- Use hostile/stress-repair candidates as contamination references.
- Use persistence and rank-coherence validation candidates as redundancy references.
- Use volume shock reversal and plain reversal references.
- Explicitly separate volatility level, volatility compression, volatility-of-volatility, and dispersion path terms.
- Predeclare h5/h10/h20 expectations so h20-only or h1-only artifacts do not drive interpretation after the fact.
- Limit the candidate set tightly; no broad state-machine parameter sweep.

## SECTION 9 - Recommended Frontier

Recommended exactly one next discovery program:

**OHLCV Volatility-of-Volatility, Dispersion Path-Dependence, and Event-Clustering Discovery Program v1**.

Core hypothesis:

Forward cross-sectional returns may depend not only on whether volatility or dispersion is high or low, but on the recent path of instability: whether volatility-of-volatility is rising or calming, whether dispersion is stabilizing or relapsing, and whether shocks arrive as isolated events or clustered sequences. This can create different information from stress repair, rank persistence, and leadership rotation if the design focuses on state paths rather than static stress gates.

Why this is the best next program:

- It is executable with existing OHLCV-derived panels and state diagnostics.
- It does not need PIT sector, industry, peer, company, or licensed security master metadata.
- It directly addresses an under-explored mechanism: instability path shape.
- It offers a disciplined way to revisit dispersion without repeating the weak dispersion acceleration refinement.
- It may explain why non-hostile leadership rotation failed: apparent leadership quality may be late-cycle crowding when instability paths are adverse.
- It can generate high information value even if negative, because it will clarify whether Project Underdog should keep investing in dispersion/volatility-state families.

Expected research risks:

- High risk of stress-repair contamination.
- Moderate risk of sparse activation from event-cluster definitions.
- Moderate risk of h10/h20 decay similar to prior dispersion work.
- Moderate risk that event clustering becomes a disguised volume-shock reversal.
- High design risk if too many state paths are enumerated.
