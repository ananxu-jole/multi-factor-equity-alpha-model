# Project Underdog - Dispersion Path-Dependence Scientific Review v1

## SECTION 1 - Executive Summary

Classification: `SCIENTIFIC_GATE_APPROVED_WITH_NOTES`

Recommendation: `ADVANCE_TO_DESIGN`

This note performs the Platform v2 scientific review for Dispersion Path-Dependence. It determines whether the mechanism is scientifically distinct enough to become a future research module.

This is a scientific review only. It does not design formulas, define candidates, specify panels, implement code, compute IC, run validation, modify governance decisions, register production candidates, change thresholds, or introduce ML.

Conclusion:

Dispersion Path-Dependence is a scientifically distinct market mechanism worthy of a Platform v2 design phase, with notes. The core phenomenon is not static dispersion level. It is the path of cross-sectional disagreement: how disagreement rises, persists, relaxes, relapses, or normalizes through time, and whether securities behave differently depending on the market's memory of prior disagreement.

The strongest rationale is that cross-sectional repricing is rarely simultaneous. Incomplete information diffusion, staggered institutional repositioning, sector rotation, asynchronous repricing, delayed consensus formation, passive-flow mechanics, and liquidity-provider balance-sheet constraints can cause the sequence of dispersion to matter more than the current level alone.

The primary scientific risk is contamination. Dispersion Path-Dependence can easily become disguised rank coherence, persistence, hostile/stress repair, volatility compression, volume shock reversal, or a rebranded version of the prior weak dispersion acceleration anchor. Therefore the design phase must carry strict orthogonality and falsifiability controls.

## SECTION 3 - Scientific Hypothesis

Dispersion Path-Dependence is the hypothesis that future cross-sectional returns depend on the recent sequence of market disagreement, not only the current magnitude of disagreement.

Market phenomenon:

- Dispersion represents cross-sectional disagreement in realized security outcomes.
- Path-dependence means the same current disagreement state may imply different forward behavior depending on how the market arrived there.
- A market moving from calm to disagreement is not equivalent to a market moving from high disagreement to orderly normalization.
- A temporary reduction in disagreement followed by relapse is not equivalent to stable normalization.
- Securities that remain orderly through repeated disagreement may carry different information from securities that only look strong in one static state.

Scientific hypothesis:

If cross-sectional disagreement has memory, then the transition path of dispersion should contain forward information beyond static dispersion level, volatility level, volatility-of-volatility, stress repair, rank persistence, rank coherence, and volume-shock reversal.

Expected direction:

- Names that remain resilient, orderly, or low-extension during constructive dispersion transitions should have positive forward relative returns.
- Names whose apparent strength is only crowded leadership, static rank persistence, or stress rebound should not be rewarded by the mechanism.

Expected horizon:

- Primary scientific expectation: h5/h10.
- Secondary expectation: h20 only as durability evidence.

This horizon choice is part of the scientific hypothesis. Prior dispersion evidence decayed at h20, so a future design should not use h20 as the sole success anchor.

## SECTION 4 - Economic Mechanism

The sequence of dispersion can matter because cross-sectional repricing is not instantaneous.

1. Incomplete information diffusion.

Different securities, sectors, and investor groups incorporate information at different speeds. A dispersion path can reveal whether disagreement is broadening because information is still being processed or narrowing because consensus is forming.

Assessment: strong economic rationale. It explains why the path of disagreement can contain information not captured by level alone.

2. Staggered institutional repositioning.

Large institutions cannot instantly rebalance. As portfolios adjust over multiple sessions, dispersion may rise, pause, relapse, or normalize depending on how flows are absorbed.

Assessment: strong rationale, especially for h5/h10. It also creates risk of overlap with participation repair and liquidity-state mechanisms, which must be controlled.

3. Sector rotation.

Dispersion paths can reflect rotation across sectors or style clusters. A market may show high disagreement while capital migrates from one group to another.

Assessment: plausible but limited under current OHLCV-only constraints. Without point-in-time sector data, the design can observe the footprint of rotation but cannot attribute it cleanly to sectors.

4. Asynchronous repricing.

Some names may move early while others lag. The path of dispersion may reveal whether repricing is spreading, exhausting, or relapsing.

Assessment: strong rationale. This is one of the cleanest economic reasons for path dependence.

5. Delayed consensus formation.

After shocks or uncertainty, market participants may converge slowly toward a new ranking of winners and losers. Dispersion normalization may be constructive if it reflects consensus formation, but adverse if it reflects crowded leadership exhaustion.

Assessment: strong but contamination-prone. It can overlap with rank coherence, persistence, or parked non-hostile leadership unless the design distinguishes disagreement path from stable rank quality.

Overall economic assessment:

The economic mechanism is credible. Dispersion Path-Dependence is not merely "dispersion is high." It asks whether the market's history of disagreement changes the meaning of present cross-sectional behavior.

## SECTION 5 - Behavioral Mechanism

Plausible participants and behaviors:

1. Institutions.

Institutions rebalance gradually because of liquidity constraints, risk limits, and execution costs. Their staggered flows can cause dispersion to persist, normalize, or relapse across several sessions.

2. ETF and passive flows.

Index and ETF flows can move baskets mechanically, while single-name repricing happens unevenly. This can create temporary disagreement between basket-level movement and security-level absorption.

3. Volatility targeting and risk-controlled allocators.

Risk-controlled investors may de-risk after volatility or dispersion rises and re-risk only after stability returns. Their behavior can create path-dependent pressure rather than one-day adjustment.

4. Liquidity providers.

Liquidity providers may widen spreads or reduce depth during disorder, then restore liquidity gradually. Securities that absorb this process without unstable extension may differ from those dependent on fragile liquidity.

5. Discretionary investors.

Discretionary investors may update conviction slowly as disagreement resolves. Delayed consensus can create h5/h10 cross-sectional differences.

6. Retail.

Retail behavior may intensify event-driven or high-attention disagreement, but it is less likely to explain broad cross-sectional dispersion path alone.

Behavioral assessment:

The strongest behavioral rationale comes from institutional repositioning, liquidity provision, and delayed consensus formation. ETF/passive flows and volatility-targeting behavior are plausible supporting channels. Retail behavior is a weaker primary explanation but may contribute around event-heavy episodes.

## SECTION 6 - Orthogonality Thesis

Orthogonality is the central gating issue.

| reference family | what is measured | why Dispersion Path-Dependence is different | contamination risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| VoV | Instability in realized volatility and volatility path behavior. | Dispersion Path-Dependence measures cross-sectional disagreement path, not instability of each security's volatility process. | Can overlap if dispersion paths are driven by volatility instability or if normalization is just volatility calming. |
| Participation Repair | Participation, breadth, or liquidity repair after adverse market states. | Dispersion Path-Dependence should not require weak breadth, hostile trend, or participation repair as the primary story. | Can become participation repair if dispersion normalization is only a proxy for breadth/liquidity healing. |
| Hostile/Stress Repair | Recovery or stabilization after panic, drawdown, liquidity stress, or hostile regimes. | Dispersion paths can occur outside panic recovery and should focus on disagreement sequence rather than drawdown repair. | High risk if high-dispersion states are mostly crisis or drawdown windows. |
| Volatility Compression | Declining volatility or stabilization after volatility stress. | Dispersion Path-Dependence concerns cross-sectional disagreement, not volatility level or compression. | Can collapse into volatility compression if dispersion normalizes only when volatility compresses. |
| Volume Shock Reversal | Reversal or stabilization after abnormal volume shocks. | Dispersion Path-Dependence concerns the market-wide path of disagreement, not a single event or volume shock. | Can overlap when dispersion paths are driven by clustered volume events or liquidity-flow reversal. |
| Persistence | Continuation of prior rank or post-drawdown rank stability. | Dispersion Path-Dependence should explain how disagreement path changes the meaning of behavior, not simply reward securities that stay strong. | Can become persistence if resilience through dispersion is just prior winners continuing. |
| Rank Coherence | Stability, consistency, or low churn in cross-sectional rank behavior. | Low churn may be a control, but the core mechanism must be disagreement path, not rank stability itself. | Very high risk if stable ranks explain most of the effect. |
| Dispersion Anchors | Static or simple-transition dispersion acceleration effects from prior work. | The new mechanism must test multi-step disagreement memory, transition, relapse, or normalization rather than simple rising/falling dispersion. | Can become a renamed version of the prior weak dispersion acceleration anchor. |
| Non-Hostile Transition | Orderly leadership rotation, breadth contribution, and non-hostile transition. | Dispersion Path-Dependence is not a leadership-continuation thesis and should not assume calm rotation predicts h20 returns. | Can drift into parked non-hostile transition if low-extension leadership language is reused without disagreement-path logic. |

Orthogonality judgment:

Dispersion Path-Dependence is scientifically distinct in concept. It measures the path, memory, and transition behavior of cross-sectional disagreement. It should advance only if design work makes that distinction operationally testable and prevents low-churn rank stability, stress repair, and volatility compression from becoming hidden primary mechanisms.

## SECTION 7 - Expected Information Content

The expected new information is not "dispersion."

The expected information is:

- path of disagreement;
- memory of disagreement;
- transition behavior after disagreement;
- whether disagreement is resolving, relapsing, or persisting;
- whether cross-sectional repricing is orderly or fragile;
- whether securities are resilient during disagreement transitions without merely being persistent winners;
- whether current dispersion level has different meaning depending on its recent path.

If successful, this family would add an intermediate layer between static state variables and candidate-level rank behavior. It would help Project Underdog distinguish:

- disagreement that is becoming informative;
- disagreement that is noise;
- disagreement that is simply stress repair;
- disagreement that is just rank coherence;
- disagreement that reflects delayed repricing or consensus formation.

## SECTION 8 - Scientific Predictions

These predictions are frozen before implementation.

Expected primary horizon:

- h5/h10.

Expected secondary horizon:

- h20 as durability evidence only, not the sole success criterion.

Expected sign:

- Positive for securities showing constructive behavior through stabilizing, resolving, or meaningfully path-dependent disagreement states.
- Negative or flat for securities whose behavior is only crowded leadership, simple rank persistence, or stress rebound.

Expected activation frequency:

- Moderate but not continuous.
- The mechanism should not be active on nearly every date, because path-dependent disagreement states should be episodic.
- The mechanism should not be so sparse that evidence is dominated by one crisis window.

Expected coverage:

- Broad enough within active dates to support cross-sectional IC interpretation.
- Not dependent on a tiny subset of names, events, or dates.

Expected strengths:

- Better scientific distinctiveness than additional participation repair variants.
- Potential h5/h10 information gain where prior dispersion h20 evidence decayed.
- Useful negative-result information even if alpha evidence is weak.
- Strong diagnostic value for whether dispersion should remain an active research axis.

Expected weaknesses:

- h20 may remain weak or decayed.
- Correlation with rank coherence or persistence may be material.
- Stress and volatility-compression contamination may appear in high-dispersion windows.
- Interpretation may be harder than more direct repair or volatility families.

## SECTION 9 - Falsifiability

The hypothesis should be rejected or revised if any of the following occur:

1. No incremental information beyond static dispersion level.

If disagreement path does not add evidence beyond current dispersion level or simple dispersion acceleration, the path-dependence hypothesis fails.

2. Complete or dominant overlap with VoV.

If dispersion path behavior is mostly explained by volatility-of-volatility, the family is not distinct enough to advance as its own module.

3. Contamination with volatility compression.

If positive behavior appears only when volatility compresses, the mechanism should be classified as volatility compression contamination.

4. Hostile/stress repair dominance.

If evidence appears only in panic, drawdown, or broad recovery windows, the mechanism should be parked or reframed as stress repair.

5. Rank coherence or persistence dominance.

If stable ranks or prior winners explain most of the effect, the mechanism fails as dispersion path-dependence.

6. Volume shock reversal contamination.

If event-heavy dispersion paths reduce to volume shock reversal or plain reversal, the module should not advance as a distinct family.

7. Unstable horizons.

If evidence is h1-only, post-hoc horizon dependent, or inconsistent across the predeclared h5/h10 focus, the hypothesis should be rejected or downgraded.

8. Sparse activation.

If active dates or active security coverage are too sparse for stable IC interpretation, the family should be parked or redesigned.

9. No consistent behavioral interpretation.

If the observed behavior cannot be tied back to delayed consensus, asynchronous repricing, institutional repositioning, or liquidity provision, the scientific thesis fails even if one metric looks positive.

10. One-window dominance.

If evidence is driven by one crisis or transition window, the module should not claim a general dispersion path mechanism.

## SECTION 10 - Learning Objective

If Dispersion Path-Dependence fails, Project Underdog will still learn:

- whether dispersion should remain an active alpha-family frontier or only a diagnostic reference;
- whether prior weak dispersion evidence failed because it used the wrong path concept or because dispersion itself lacks robust signal value;
- whether h5/h10 is the natural horizon for dispersion mechanisms, with h20 decay as a structural limitation;
- whether rank coherence and persistence already capture the useful part of orderly cross-sectional structure;
- whether high-dispersion behavior is inseparable from hostile/stress repair in the current OHLCV-only universe;
- whether future dispersion research requires external PIT sector, industry, or peer-relative metadata rather than OHLCV-only state paths.

This learning objective is sufficient to justify a design phase even if eventual IC evidence is weak.

## SECTION 11 - Research Risks

| risk | assessment | mitigation required in design |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Redundancy risk | High versus rank coherence, persistence, and prior dispersion anchors. | Predefine contamination references and require incremental path evidence beyond level and rank stability. |
| Contamination risk | High versus hostile/stress repair and volatility compression. | Separate disagreement path from panic recovery and volatility calming. |
| Implementation complexity | Moderate. Path states are more complex than static signals. | Keep the design narrow and avoid broad state-machine enumeration. |
| Interpretation difficulty | Moderate to high. Multiple market structures can produce similar dispersion paths. | Require economic and behavioral attribution for each design concept during the later design phase. |
| Overfitting risk | High if many path definitions are tried. | Limit candidate budget and freeze horizons before implementation. |
| OHLCV limitations | Material. OHLCV can observe dispersion footprints but not true sector, flow, or ownership causes. | Keep claims at the observable market-behavior level and avoid causal overstatement. |

Overall risk assessment:

The research risk is material but acceptable for a Platform v2 design phase because the mechanism has high information value and clear falsification criteria.

## SECTION 12 - Recommendation

Recommendation: `ADVANCE_TO_DESIGN`

Scientific gate classification: `SCIENTIFIC_GATE_APPROVED_WITH_NOTES`

Rationale:

- The mechanism is conceptually distinct from static dispersion, VoV, volatility compression, stress repair, rank coherence, persistence, and volume shock reversal.
- The economic mechanism is plausible: disagreement paths can arise from incomplete information diffusion, staggered institutional repositioning, asynchronous repricing, sector/style rotation, and delayed consensus formation.
- The behavioral mechanism is plausible: institutional flows, liquidity providers, risk-controlled allocators, ETF/passive flows, and discretionary investors can adjust at different speeds.
- The expected information content is meaningful: path of disagreement, memory of disagreement, and transition behavior.
- The learning value is high even if the module fails.

Notes attached to approval:

- The design phase must not define formulas until the scientific predictions and falsification criteria in this note are preserved.
- The design phase must not broaden into event clustering, VoV, or participation repair unless separately scoped.
- The design phase must explicitly compare against static dispersion anchors, rank coherence, persistence, hostile/stress repair, volatility compression, volume shock reversal, VoV, and parked non-hostile transition.
- h5/h10 should be the primary scientific horizon expectation; h20 should be durability evidence only.
- Any design that relies on stable ranks as the primary driver should be rejected as rank-coherence contamination.
